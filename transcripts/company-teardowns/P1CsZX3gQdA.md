[04:41] Continue watching is the important task for HBO Go users. Leaving technical sync problems aside, continue section was difficult to differentiate from the rest of the page. So they decided to make the tiles stand out so they appear above the fold on the majority of computer screens. They also made it easier to play the next episode straight from the series page. 

[05:13] Next problem is the platforms menu. The platforms menu structure was too complicated with unnecessary levels and too many sub menus. The old menu version had three main sub menus with even more tertiary levels. When testing the old version, it became clear that users have a hard time finding what they are looking for. Let it be actual content or user specific settings. 

[05:44] So they took every element of the menu and printed them one by one. During the card sorting tests, the users were asked to group these items and also give them a name. After a couple of sessions, it was obvious to group items in six main categories. Recommendations in home, movies, series, notifications, settings, and help. 

[06:14] Also, during testing, the users wanted my list to be at hand. Even though the my list feature is very popular in streaming services, currently it is almost impossible to find in HBO Go. While the platform is pushing generic content to the users, so they decided to make this functionality easily accessible from the main navigation and split into series and movies. 

[06:45] They also discovered that watching series and movies are two completely different use cases. So, they decided to split it into movies and series tabs. Next problem is regarding home screen. [07:16] from the recommended content. However, users were not engaging with the content as much as HBO would have liked it. They rarely opened the menu or navigated to different pages. The editorial recommended section became much smaller and instead, they shifted the focus to the important content, continue watching, watch list, 

[07:47] recommended for you, etc. So, when users logged in, they immediately found the content they were looking for. In the old application, playing an episode took six taps to complete. Since this is one of the main use of the application, they wanted to make this 

[08:17] user journey significantly shorter. Since users are more likely to scroll than navigate to new pages, so they created a layout for the series detail pages, where users would find seasons and episodes right under each other. Also, they integrated a little help. The application remembers where the user left, so instead of six steps, users can now play an episode with a simple scroll and a tap.
