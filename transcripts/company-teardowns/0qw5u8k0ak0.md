[02:34] to start learning a language is easy. [03:07] mechanism borrowed directly from gaming. The sunk cost and loss aversion psychology that makes people unable to break a chain once they have started one. Dolingo users have reported rescheduling flights, cutting short evenings out, and opening the app in hospital waiting rooms rather than lose their streak. [03:37] leaned into its menacing quality. The brand deliberately cultivated the joke that Duo would come for you if you missed a lesson. What started as a meme became a marketing strategy. [07:20] There are three things Dolingo does that the best product builders in any industry understand. Lesson one, design for the behavior you want, not the outcome you want. Dualingo does not measure success by how many people become fluent in French. It measures success by daily active users and streak retention. If you can get someone to show up every day, the learning happens as a consequence. 

[07:50] Think about what you are building. What is the one behavior that if your users did it consistently would lead to the outcome you are both after? Build everything around that behavior. Lesson two, give your product permission to have a personality. Dolingo's green owl is not just a mascot. It is a brand relationship. The willingness to be slightly threatening, to be in on the joke, to let the product 

[08:22] have genuine character drove the social media verality that no advertising budget could have bought. Most products are afraid of personality. They smooth out anything that might feel unsafe. Dolingo leaned into the unsafe version and found billions of dollars of free marketing waiting there. Lesson three, the premium model only works if the free tier is genuinely excellent. 

[08:54] Many companies build a free tier that is intentionally limited to push people toward paid. Dolingo built a free tier that is genuinely worldclass and then let a small percentage of users choose to pay for convenience.
