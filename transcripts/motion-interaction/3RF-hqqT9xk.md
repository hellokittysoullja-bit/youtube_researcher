Because I’m Bob, this is where we  learn how to have more fun playing RPGs together, and to make your own D&D  campaign, start with these questions: WHERE is everybody? WHO are they? WHAT do they need/want? WHY don’t they have it? Then to answer those questions quickly, just  use some random tables I made! And sure, it

Then if you want, you can spend the  rest of your life adding more and more layers of detail that your  players will never know exists! Starting with WHERE: I like to  think of any adventure’s setting as a combo of a big region with  a primary or starting location. For the region, think of Earth’s major  biomes: aquatic, grassland, forest, desert, and tundra. But we put those last  two together because adventuring in extreme 

[01:02] temperatures is rarely as fun as we think it  will be, and these barren biomes don’t have as many distinct variations as the others.  Then roll 2d4, we got our region. Awesome! Then for a starting location, we want our  main characters to begin in a settlement and then venture into the wilderness! So we’ll  circle back to the wilderness part. Right now, just pick a settlement, like a  city, town, village, or camp. —and an establishment where the characters  are hanging out right now! Like a tavern, 

[01:32] smithy, trading post, or at home! So  that’s two more d4s, and we’re good! D&D does not include those instructions, but  for D&D or just about any game you want to play, you can search online for free premade characters  or for an automatic random character generator! Whichever method, make sure your players  customize some details so they feel a connection to their characters. Then have them  decide how these unique fantasy characters 

[03:11] all know each other, and why they’re  coming together for this adventure! As for the bad guys, we just need a boss  with some minions. Any of these 12 pairs of baddies work perfectly fine for a  fantasy adventure. Roll it up…done. But not really, because we  also need a third party: the contenders! These 1d20 types  of guys are not necessarily evil, but they may have their reasons for siding  with the bad guys or with the good guys or for staying neutral, depending on what those  other parties want and how they try to get it… 

[03:45] …which is #3, WHAT do they  want?? For the bad guys, keep it simple by starting with what they NEED: The Artefact, Knowledge,  Money, Servants, Food/Fuel, or Status. This is the thing that they  NEED to get what they really WANT! Love, Revenge, Transcendence, Destruction, Conquest, Legacy, Perfection,  Control, Terror, Expression. This WANT can even be used as an  underlying theme for your campaign, and I think this particular list will  help you create a good villain: as in, 

[04:18] a complex but not complicated  bad guy. Cus check this out! *Warning for new game masters: Your bad guy  does not need a traumatic backstory or a morally gray motivation behind their actions. A) It’s  difficult to truly pull that off unless you’re a professional writer, and B) it might spoil  the players’ fun when you turn defeating the bad guy into a philosophical debate rather  than a simple matter of heroic justice! This is just my opinion, but at the end of the  day, I think it is both easier and more fun to create a bad guy who deserves to get punched in  the face simultaneously by all of your good guys. 

[04:48] And yeah, for the good guys: they WANT to  stop the bad guy because their evil actions, directly or indirectly, are hurting or will hurt  innocent people or wildlife. But for one or two of the player characters, make it personal: One of  their family members or friends was threatened, captured, or killed; or their  home was or will be destroyed! This connection is your strongest hook, and it has more weight if you let  the player choose it themself. Finally, Why don’t they already have what they  want? This obstacle is usually one of four things: 

[05:24] Geography - The knowledge they seek is kept  on the highest mountain peak in the region. Opposition - There’s hordes of dangerous  animals or an army in their way Timing - They can’t perform the  ritual until the blood moon rises! Or tools - they need this other thing  before they can access the thing they need to get the thing they really  want. A fetch quest, if you will. Often you’ll want to come up with a handful of  obstacles because some should only apply to the good guys, some only to the bad guys, but some  may apply to both parties, and possibly even to 

[05:55] those contenders we talked about earlier.  Also this is how we circle back to where: Where is the boss and where exactly  is this thing that they need? Pick a new region and a new settlement  or one of these wilderness sites. Now putting it all into action  with some random results! Our good guys start in an Aquatic region,  specifically on or near a Reef. In a City, at Home. Our bad guy boss is an Alien with  Colonist minions, and we also have a party of 

[06:27] Wolves in the mix here. The boss needs Food/Fuel  because they want Perfection, but the obstacles are Timing and Geography, so let’s say the  aliens are waiting for a Fall from Power and the heroes have to deal with Magical Corruption.  And where are these aliens? A prairie, in a cave. So using those random results, here’s the  campaign premise I scribbled out as fast as possible: These aliens crash landed  in this prairie hundreds of years ago, but the alien ruler was like, “it’s  fine, we’ll just live here now.” So they’ve been inhabiting and colonizing  the caves under this grassland for centuries, 

[07:00] only causing some problems  with our local wildlife, the wolves of the prairie who  live in some of these caves. But the bad guy, our boss alien, wants more;  they don’t want to live underground anymore, they want perfection; specifically,  they want to transform this prairie to be like their former home,  and to do it, they need fuel: which happens to be the calcified ocean life  that makes up the reef where our heroes live. What’s the science behind that? I have no  idea. It doesn't matter. Because now our 

[07:31] heroes’ Home/Wildlife is threatened by the boss  and minion aliens, and the party’s obstacles are the magical corruption of the reef, the alien  colonist minions performing that corruption, and the wolves which are now being pushed out  of the prairie by the increased alien activity. And finally, when our alien boss collects  enough of this corrupted reef food/fuel, they will be able to demonstrate their power  and resources and this promise of perfection to their people, driving the downfall of the  current peaceful alien ruler, and taking over! 

[08:03] That right there’s enough conflict to drive  at least 5 to 10 sessions of gameplay. Think of all the gameplay you could have just in the  reef city with rumors of encroaching wolves? Now it’s just a matter of fleshing out a few  notable NPCs and choice locations through which your heroes will experience  this world. But even that’s easy! When your player asks for the name of  an NPC, make them come up with one! Then just don’t forget about  plugging in some interesting 

[08:38] treasure and other rewards  when the heroes deserve it.
