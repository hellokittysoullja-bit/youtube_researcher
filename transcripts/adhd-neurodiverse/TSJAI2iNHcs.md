Maybe you find it hard to pay attention in class or control your  emotions when things don't go your way. If so, you might be dealing with something  called executive function deficits. Today, we're going to learn about executive  functions and how to improve them. We'll cover: • What executive functions are • Signs that you might have executive function deficits • And strategies to boost your executive function skills 

[00:35] What Are Executive Functions? Executive functions are like the control  center of your brain. They help you plan, focus, remember things, and control your  actions and emotions. Think of them as your brain's superhero team, working together to help  you succeed in school, at home, and with friends. Some kids find these skills more challenging  than others. That's okay! Everyone's brain develops differently, and there are lots of  ways to improve your executive functions. The following are 9 signs that you  might have executive function deficits: 

[01:06] 1) You often forget to bring important things  to school, like your homework or lunch. 2) You have trouble with starting tasks, whether  they're big projects or small everyday tasks. 3) You struggle to manage your time and often run  late or rush to finish things at the last minute. 4) You have trouble paying attention,  even when you try really hard. 5) Your backpack, desk, or room is always messy,  and you can't find things when you need them. 6) When faced with a problem, you have a hard time 

[01:37] with flexible thinking or seeing  things from a new perspective. 7) You tend to give up quickly when tasks become  challenging, even if they’re important to you. 8) You get upset easily when plans  change or things don't go your way. 9) It's difficult for you to wait  your turn or control your impulses. Do any of these sound familiar? If so, don't  worry! There are many strategies you can use to boost your executive function  skills. Let’s explore some of them. 10 Strategies To Boost Your  Executive Function Skills 

[02:10] 1) Use visual reminders. Create a colorful chart  or use sticky notes to help you remember important tasks. You can make a morning checklist to make  sure you have everything you need for school, or a task list to help you remember what  chores or homework you need to complete. 2) Break big tasks into smaller steps.  If cleaning your room feels overwhelming, try breaking it down. Start with just  picking up clothes from the floor, then move on to organizing your closet,  then move on to cleaning your desk. 

[02:40] 3) Set timers. Use a timer to help you focus  on a task for a specific amount of time. Start with short periods, like 5 or 10 minutes, and  gradually increase as you get better at focusing. 4) Create a homework station. Set up a  special place for doing homework with all the supplies you need. This can  help you stay organized and focused. 5) Use planners or calendars. Write  down your assignments, due dates, and activities. Check your planner  every day to stay on top of your tasks. 

[03:14] 6) Practice mindfulness. Take a few deep  breaths when you're feeling overwhelmed or frustrated. This can help you  calm down and think more clearly. 7) Get moving. Regular exercise,  like running, jumping, or dancing, can help improve your focus and self-control. 8) Play games that challenge your  brain. Puzzles, memory games, and strategy games can help strengthen your  executive function skills while having fun. 9) Use "self-talk." When you're facing  a challenge, try talking to yourself in 

[03:47] a positive way. For example, "I can do  this if I take it one step at a time." 10) Ask for help. Don't be afraid  to ask your parents, teachers, or friends for help when you need it. They can  offer support and ideas to make things easier. Remember, improving your executive  function skills is like building a muscle – it takes practice and patience.  Some days will be easier than others, but the important thing is to keep trying and  celebrate your progress, no matter how small. 

[04:19] Here are some encouraging  thoughts to keep in mind: • Everyone has strengths and weaknesses. Your executive function skills are  just one part of who you are. • Your brain is still growing and changing.  Executive function skills often improve naturally as you grow older. And with practice  and effort, you can speed up your progress. • There are many successful people who have  struggled with executive functions. They have found ways to work around their  challenges and achieve great things. So, the next time you're facing an executive  function challenge, remember these steps: • Recognize the difficulty you're having
