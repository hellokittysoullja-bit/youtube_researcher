# Neurodiversity-Informed Online Course Design - Cognitive Load
Instructional By Design · 5 мин · 16 просмотров · en · https://youtu.be/PLgsm-bVbIo



[00:00] Neurodeiversity informed online course design. Cognitive load. Cognitive load is an important factor that influences learning. It is the amount of mental effort needed to process information and complete tasks at a specific time and within the limits of working memory. It describes how students take in, organize, and use information, recognizing that working memory can only handle a finite amount at once. When cognitive load is well 

[00:33] managed, students can focus their mental resources on meaningful content. When cognitive load is excessive, it can overwhelm working memory, making it harder for students to understand, retain, and apply new information. This is especially important for neurode divergent students who may be more affected by high cognitive demands. Traditional online course designs can unintentionally increase cognitive load through confusing navigation, inconsistent layouts, or inaccessible 

[01:05] content. These design issues can place disproportionate strain on neurodeivergent learners, making it harder for them to keep up, stay organized, and fully engage with the material. The triarchic theory of cognitive load distinguishes three types of mental load placed on working memory. Intrinsic, extraneous and germanine. Intrinsic load is the mental effort required to understand the core content of a task. 

[01:35] It relates to the inherent complexity of the material itself, prior knowledge and expertise and degree of abstractness. For example, concrete examples have a lower intrinsic load than abstract ones. Intrinsic load can be managed by sequencing and scaffolding. Extraneous load refers to the mental effort required by how information is presented or tasks are designed and does not contribute to learning. For example, unclear instructions or unnecessary 

[02:07] distractions. Extraneous cognitive load often results from suboptimal instructional design, poor navigation or ineffective multimedia elements. Extraneous load can be managed by removing distractions and reviewing instructions. Germany load is the mental effort required to process information and build knowledge structures, organizing of ideas and forming connections. Germaine load can be managed by content 

[02:38] maps, summaries, and explanations. Inclusivine course design should aim to decreasing extraneous cognitive load and increasing germaine cognitive load. Intensified extraneous cognitive load disproportionately affects neurode divergent students. Poorly designed online courses can impose avoidable extraneous load by 

[03:09] forcing learners to attend to multiple information sources simultaneously known as split attention and exacerbate difficulties in attention processing and sensory load. Several critical areas can impact cognitive load in online learning, including design, delivery, environment, instruction, and support. How can these factors negatively impact cognitive load? 

[03:41] Course design, poor layout, and inconsistent navigation. Examples: multiple folders with vague titles like module stuff or folders labeled as readings, duplicate links, or important materials buried in long announcements. Delivery, pacing, and modality. Examples: content covered fast with no opportunity for questions. Lectures have background noise, low volume, or unclear screen sharing where text is too small 

[04:12] to read. Slides are packed with dense text while the instructor is simultaneously speaking different information. Learning environment, flexibility, and predictability. Examples: Due dates for major assignments are close together. Late submissions are not accepted or weekly activities have inconsistent due dates and times. Instruction, task complexity, scaffolding, and assessment structure. Examples, jumping to complex content 

[04:43] without assessing understanding of basic concepts and definitions, descriptions and instructions for activities and assignments are unclear. And finally, academic and technological support such as clear guidance and responsive assistance. Examples, taking 5 days to reply to a student with a question on how to create a thread in the discussion forum or not including clear and easy to find information for tech support. All of these areas play a key role in managing overall cognitive load. 

[05:19] Carefully evaluate every element of online learning environments. Determine its effect on cognitive loads and decide whether to include the element. Sensory overload is related to course design, not a student deficit.
