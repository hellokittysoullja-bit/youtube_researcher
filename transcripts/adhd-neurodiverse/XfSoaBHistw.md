[01:06] That is where universal design of learning  (or UDL) becomes a part of the conversation. UDL can provide a framework for inclusive  teaching and learning materials and pedagogy. We are currently redesigning this FSHN course  to ensure lectures, discussions, visual aids, videos, printed materials, and more are accessible  to, usable by, and inclusive of all students. Universal design is built on the core belief  that we should design environments to be 

[01:36] useable by all people “without the need  for adaptation or specialized design.” This inclusive approach allows everyone to benefit  from such designs. Many students may want to use closed captioning on videos regardless of  hearing ability. For anyone able to read, captioning could help assimilate  difficult scientific concepts and tap into the brain differently  than hearing the words alone. UDL applies this same philosophy of  universal design to every aspect of learning and takes into consideration  learning spaces, materials, instruction, 

[02:09] assessment, as well as course  culture and behavior management. UDL is an inclusive educational framework  that is built around cognitive neuroscience at its core. It attempts to remove learning  barriers while maintaining course rigor. While my team and I redesign  FSHN 120 with UDL in mind, we seek to create a course where  all students feel heard, welcomed, and cared for. We are striving to ensure that  neurodiversity is celebrated. We hope that we 

[02:40] are universally accessible without the need for  a LOA. This course is being flipped on its side and examined from a student-centered  approach focusing on student agency. A little history lesson…. In the 1990’s, Dr. David  Rose and the Center for Applied Special Technology (CAST) articulated the three UDL principles: #1: Multiple means of engagement that focus on the affective networks or the Why of Learning #2: Multiple means of representation which focus 

[03:12] on the recognition network or the What of Learning #3: Multiple means of action and expression which focus on the strategic  networks or the How of Learning So what does this look like in FSHN 120? Well, we  are still figuring this out! But, we are listening to a student advisory board made of your peers.  They are helping us to create a course focusing on student interests, agency, and motivation.  Our goal is that you become self-directed, choosing how you wish to learn the materials and  in what setting. You choose to either take this 

[03:46] course online or in person. You can choose to  engage with our team in the online discussion, the virtual tutoring center, during personal  office hours, or you don’t have to engage at all.
