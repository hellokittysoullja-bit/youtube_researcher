[00:01] Hello everyone. In this video, I will talk about one of the principles of composition. It is balance. Balance, in essence, is a visually pleasing integration of elements in a layout. Generally, balance occurs when visual elements within a composition are evenly distributed to convey a sense of stability, harmony, and equilibrium. Balance can be dynamic, formal, or radial. Formal balance, or symmetry, is the simplest type of balance. It is widely used in architecture because it symbolizes stability and longevity. 

[00:32] Formal balance is based on the mirror reflection of an image. If we divide this building into two equal vertical parts, we will see that both sides are identical to each other and are a mirror reflection. You can create formal balance by evenly distributing visual elements on both sides of a composition. This creates a sense of stability and static order. In this case, both sides of the composition are identical and mirror each other. Dynamic balance, or asymmetry. This type of balance is more interesting and 

[01:04] , at the same time, more complex to execute. It occurs when layout elements are distributed unevenly, making their arrangement seem random and dynamic. Even though dynamic balance looks more chaotic compared to formal balance, it still requires much more careful thought and analysis to create a sense of visual balance. This means we cannot just scatter elements across the layout randomly. We must arrange them precisely so that no visual imbalance occurs on one side or the other. Radial 

[01:35] balance. The basis of radial balance is a circle. Radial balance essentially occurs when elements of a composition radiate from a central point in a circle. This type of balance allows for the creation of a clearly defined center of composition. A graphic designer can use contrast in color, value, shape, and texture to balance or , conversely, highlight visual elements in a composition. Let's take a closer look at these elements. Color is one of the most powerful visual elements in graphic design. It is used to attract 

[02:05] attention, to visually separate objects , and as an additional means of conveying meaning. A color composition will always be more appealing to the viewer than a monochrome one. The term value describes the lightness or darkness of a composition element. For example, a black circle on a white background creates a sharper contrast and has more visual weight than a gray circle on white. Thus, smaller objects that have high contrast can balance larger objects with lower contrast. A 

[02:38] shape represents a two-dimensional plane that is bounded by a closed contour line, color, value, texture, or font. A shape has width and height, but not depth. Small complex shapes can balance larger and simpler ones. Shapes can be geometric, organic, or random. They can be used everywhere. both for creating various compositions and for countless patterns. Texture refers to the appearance or feel of a surface. Texture can be felt through touch or 

[03:09] conveyed by visual means. It can be velvety, glossy, fluffy, leathery, rough, and so on. Texture, like any other visual element, can convey different emotions and also complement the semantic content of a composition. Small elements with vivid textures can balance out large elements with smooth and calm textures. Visual balance is influenced not only by the size of composition elements but also by their inherent lightness or darkness. This is commonly referred to as the visual 

[03:39] weight of an element. A visually heavier element will be more noticeable than a lighter element of the same size . When elements of a composition have the same visual weight, they are in balance. A small element positioned far from the center of a composition can balance a larger element that is closer to the center. This serves as the foundation for creating asymmetrical balance. The reader's gaze can be directed to a specific point in a layout if all elements are arranged correctly. For example, if all shapes are positioned in one direction, the 

[04:10] reader's gaze will follow that direction. Remember that visual balance must be maintained in all areas of graphic design. Balance reflects not just a state of physical equilibrium, but also a state of visual harmony, where all elements of a composition are interconnected and correlated.
