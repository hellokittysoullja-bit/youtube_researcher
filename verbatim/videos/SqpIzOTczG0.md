# The Streak Was Never a Reward. It Was a Threat.

**Канал:** The Manipulated Mind · 8 мин · субтитры: en (авто YouTube) · https://youtu.be/SqpIzOTczG0

> «Слова автора» — дословный текст из транскрипта (убраны только um/uh и заикания; в авто-субтитрах мало знаков препинания). «Мысль» и «Для Напарника» — вывод разметчика, это не речь автора.


---

## 1. Стрик задуман как угроза, а не награда
`стрики · этика · тёмные-паттерны · мотивация` · [00:33](https://youtu.be/SqpIzOTczG0?t=33)

**Мысль:** Награды можно игнорировать, угрозы нельзя; защита уже имеющегося тянет сильнее, чем погоня за новым. Это измеримая особенность мозга.

**Слова автора:**

> Not how do we reward people for coming back tomorrow? A different question, quieter and colder. How do we make people afraid of what happens if they do not? Because rewards are optional. You can walk away from a reward and feel fine. Threats are not optional. Something in you refuses to just let them sit there. Think about the last time you protected something you already had, compared to the last time you worked hard to get something brand new. Which one pulled at you harder? Which one kept you up later, phone in hand, just to be safe, just to check one more time? If you are honest with yourself, protecting the thing you already possessed felt more urgent than chasing something you did not have yet. That is not a quirk of your personality. That is a documented, measurable feature of how the human brain values loss compared to gain.

**Для Напарника:** не применимо: стрик как угроза противоречит тону «ноль, а не минус». Напарник показывает накопленное и не наказывает за пропуск.

## 2. Теория перспектив: потери вдвое больнее, поэтому сгорающий счётчик давит
`стрики · этика · нейро · исследования` · [01:47](https://youtu.be/SqpIzOTczG0?t=107)

**Мысль:** Потери ощущаются примерно вдвое сильнее эквивалентных приобретений; стрик Snapchat превратил это в продуктовую механику со счётчиком, который может исчезнуть.

**Слова автора:**

> In 1979, psychologists Daniel Kahneman and Amos Tversky published a paper called prospect theory, work that would eventually reshape entire industries far beyond academic economics. Their central finding was simple to state and strange to actually live inside. Losses feel roughly twice as painful as equivalent gains feel good. Losing $20 stings more than finding $20 ever delights you. This asymmetry now has a name inside behavioral science. Loss aversion. For decades, that finding mostly sat inside academic journals and university lecture halls, cited by economists, largely unknown outside them. Then Silicon Valley found it, and Silicon Valley does not leave good research sitting on a shelf. At Stanford, researcher B.J. Fogg spent years studying what made small daily behaviors repeat automatically, running experiments inside what became known as the persuasive technology lab. Fogg's models on behavior triggers were later studied directly by early product designers at Facebook and Instagram, some of whom sat in his classroom before ever writing a line of production code. But loss aversion, on its own, is only a finding. It needed a to become an actual product feature. It needed something a person could build, something that could visibly break, something a user could watch and worry about in real time. In 2015, Snapchat shipped a small yellow flame icon that appeared next to a friend's name. The streak. Two users sending photos back and forth every single day, a counter climbing higher with no visible ceiling. Snapchat's own public messaging around the feature emphasized connection and consistency between friends. But underneath that framing, the design does something loss aversion research predicts with unsettling precision. A counter that only climbs upward does not feel urgent on its own.

**Для Напарника:** Счётчики на острове только растут и не сгорают: остров не «увядает» при пропуске, что снимает давление страха потери.

## 3. Endowed progress и пик паники перед концом окна
`стрики · время · психология · этика` · [03:36](https://youtu.be/SqpIzOTczG0?t=216)

**Мысль:** Подаренный стартовый прогресс защищают сильнее, а срочность взлетает только в последние минуты окна; поэтому о стрике вспоминают в 23:57.

**Слова автора:**

> A counter that can vanish overnight does. Researchers Joseph Nunes and Xavier Drèze had already documented a closely related effect years earlier in a 2006 study describing what they named the endowed progress effect. Give someone partial credit toward a goal they did not personally earn, and they will fight harder to protect that head start than someone starting from true zero ever would. A number simply handed to you, even one you never chose or asked for, starts to feel like property the moment it exists in front of you. And losing property, unlike missing an opportunity, feels closer to theft. Put those two findings together, and what you get is no longer a game. It is a countdown wearing the costume of a reward. There is a third piece worth naming because it explains why the panic hits exactly when it does. Behavioral researchers studying deadline pressure have long noted that urgency does not build gradually. It stays flat, almost invisible, until the very end of a window, then spikes sharply in the final moments before that window closes. That is why you do not think about your streak at noon. You think about it at 11:57 p.m., heart rate slightly elevated, over a photo you do not even care about. The app did not need to remind you constantly. It only needed to make sure the ending felt close enough to hurt.

**Для Напарника:** Endowed progress можно использовать мягко (остров стартует с первым ориентиром), но без дедлайна «до полуночи».

## 4. Удержание против вовлечения: страх вместо желания, платная заморозка
`стрики · удержание · этика · стыд` · [04:53](https://youtu.be/SqpIzOTczG0?t=293)

**Мысль:** Стрик-механики описывают как инструмент retention, не engagement: он требует бояться потери, а не хотеть вернуться. Duolingo продаёт защиту от тревоги, созданной самим продуктом.

**Слова автора:**

> But here is the dark turn. Internal design documentation later reported from inside several major platforms describe streak-style mechanics explicitly as retention tools, not engagement tools, in the language used by the teams that built them. That distinction matters more than it sounds like it should. Engagement asks you to want to come back. Retention does not actually require you to want anything at all. It only requires you to fear the alternative more than you resent the app itself. Teenagers, in particular, have reported feeling obligated to maintain streaks with friends they had already grown distant from, sometimes handing over their own passwords so someone else could log in on their behalf during a vacation, an illness, or a period of grief. The friendship, in some of these cases, had already quietly ended. The number had not. And the number was the thing still demanding daily attention. Some apps went further, turning the fear itself into a purchase. Duolingo, founded by Luis von Ahn, built its own streak system on nearly identical logic, and eventually introduced a paid streak freeze, letting users buy protection against the very anxiety the feature was designed to produce in the first place. The company was not simply selling language lessons anymore. It was selling relief from a threat it had built into the product on purpose.

**Для Напарника:** не применимо: платная защита стрика и страх потери противоречат принципам Напарника; метрика должна быть желанием вернуться, а не страхом.
