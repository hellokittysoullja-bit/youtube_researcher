## 1. 80–90% усилий на первый экран: именно там самые большие скачки
`лендинг · конверсия · метрики` · [03:36](https://youtu.be/zA0B-VwOPn4?t=216)

**Мысль:** Первый экран видят все, а 60% не листают дальше; основные выигрыши в тестах дают заголовок и картинка.

**Слова автора:**

> Most important section on your entire landing page is the above the fold. This is the topmost section of any page that is immediately visible without scrolling, and it is the only section that 100% of your users will see, but it's also the only section that 60% of people statistically will never even scroll past. This is why Alex Hormozi suggests spending 80 to 90% of your efforts just on the above the fold. >> Almost all of the juice that's going to come, the biggest jumps in conversion rate, happen above the fold. That is where you're selling them on whether they're going to stay on the page and continue to read more, and that's where the biggest returns will come from. I would say 90% of our tests are either headline or image tests, and that's it.

**Для Напарника:** Лендинг Напарника: сначала довести до ума заголовок, подзаголовок, CTA и hero-картинку (кот и остров), потом остальное.

## 2. Заголовок сразу называет желаемый результат и отвечает «зачем мне это»; приём «чтобы»
`лендинг · тексты · конверсия` · [04:30](https://youtu.be/zA0B-VwOPn4?t=270)

**Мысль:** Заголовок, повторяющий название компании или описывающий, чем занимаются, проигрывает; формула «делаем X, чтобы Y, чтобы Z» дала +64% конверсии.

**Слова автора:**

> The above the fold primarily consists of a headline, subheadline, CTA button, and a hero image. So, now what we're going to do is take Alex Hormozi's well-known value equation framework and apply it to not only the above the fold, but to the entire landing page altogether. So, the first part of his equation is the dream outcome, which we want to immediately articulate in the headline in the top part of the website and instantly answer the question of why should I care? This sounds super simple, but I cannot tell you how many pages that do the exact opposite of what Alex is saying, where they simply just repeat the company name, they have vague copy that no one really understands, or they simply just explain who they are and what they do. >> the dream outcome, which is what's the big thing that people want. So, in this instance, it'd be they want to make more money in their gym, they might want to lose weight, they might want to have a fixed their relationship, they might want to look cool in front of their friends, whatever. >> I like to use the so that principle. So, for the solar panel cleaning business, their before headline just simply stated what they do, which is exterior cleaning services. Using the so that principle, we offer exterior cleaning so that your solar panels can produce more energy, so that you can cut your energy bill, so that you can actually feel confident your solar is working. And we captured that end result perfectly in this headline here and saw a 64% lift in conversions.

**Для Напарника:** Заголовок лендинга: не «приложение для фокуса», а «Сядь рядом с котом на 5 минут — и дело сдвинется, чтобы не стыдиться откладывания».

## 3. Hero-картинка должна визуально доказывать обещание заголовка
`лендинг · иерархия` · [05:48](https://youtu.be/zA0B-VwOPn4?t=348)

**Мысль:** Изображение показывает результат, который обещан текстом, и тема результата повторяется по всей странице.

**Слова автора:**

> So, the next thing Alex says is that your hero image should also visually represent your dream outcome. Most landing pages suck. Here's how to have them suck less. The hero image should add proof to the headline and subheadline. So, either I'm showing you the thing you're going to get that or the outcome that you want to experience. >> If your headline is promising a luxury high-end closet, your image should actually show that. If it's promising a straighter, brighter smile, I should see that in your image. If it's promising smoother, brighter skin, again, I should see that visually. The dream outcome should not only be in the above the fold, but it should also be throughout your entire landing page in the headlines and imagery.

**Для Напарника:** Hero: остров, который вырос, и кот рядом — как визуальное доказательство обещания «прогресс виден».

## 4. Социальное доказательство: визуальное, «стеной», на виду, не в карусели
`лендинг · доверие · конверсия` · [06:42](https://youtu.be/zA0B-VwOPn4?t=402)

**Мысль:** Текстовые отзывы заезжены; скриншоты, фото и видео убедительнее. Стек из разных форматов создаёт доверие. Скрытое в карусели не видят.

**Слова автора:**

> The issue is that today, everyone uses social proof so much so that people have become very skeptical to anything they see online. Basic text reviews, for example, are super overused and literally everyone does them, and so, people don't trust this on its own anymore. Second, is you want it to be as visual as possible. So, just a bunch of words on a screen is less compelling than a screenshot of someone's bank account after they made money. Or, someone saying, "Hey, I lost 20 lb" is not as compelling as the picture of them losing 20 lb, which is also less compelling than a video of them weighing in, and then a video of them weighing out. >> Alex's approach instead is to be as intentional as possible through your social proof and to show it as visually as much as possible through images or video. This helps create an emotional connection that text on its own cannot achieve, and it just makes your proof feel way, way more impactful. I can personally attest to what Alex is saying here as we've seen conversion increases upwards of 50 to 70% just by using this one strategy. This luxury closet brand shows off the beautiful units that were installed in conjunction with the written reviews to really help sell the dream. For this fitness offer, you can see visuals of the before and after in combination with a little blurb talking about the person's journey. This cosmetic dentistry brand shows off the before and after images of the results in conjunction with the reviews. The other thing that Alex is super big on is stacking social proof. >> If you look at Yelp, you look at Google, you look at Facebook, all of these have reviews for your business. And so, I would go into the stars, you click into it, and there's like a hundred of them. But, if I screenshot a hundred of those, and then I frame them, and I put them on my lobby wall from floor to ceiling, it's overwhelming the amount of proof that is. So, to execute this visually, you can leverage a wall of love format where you basically shower people with so much stuff, right? Video reviews, images, and text reviews all in one so that they're already sold. You want to show all of your reviews up front, especially on mobile, and you never want to hide it in a carousel because statistically, carousels have very low engagement rates, and typically, whatever is hidden does not get seen. You basically want people to consume the vast amount of social proof you have and subconsciously build so much trust that they're already sold.

**Для Напарника:** Отзывы на лендинге выкладывать открыто (скриншоты чатов, истории), а не каруселью; с реальных людей.

## 5. Снижай риск, время до результата и усилие: гарантии под CTA, сроки в заголовке, 3–4 шага
`лендинг · доверие · конверсия · запуск-задачи` · [08:39](https://youtu.be/zA0B-VwOPn4?t=519)

**Мысль:** Бейджи и гарантии под кнопкой снимают страх (+30% в тесте); срок «за 2 недели» закрывает разрыв до результата; подзаголовок делает путь лёгким; больше трёх-четырёх шагов повышает воспринимаемое усилие.

**Слова автора:**

> Alex is a big proponent of risk reversals. >> I buy stuff, and they guarantee it, or they make it risk-free, or they show me tons of proof, I'm more likely to pay for it and pay more for it. >> Things like money-back guarantees, return policies, warranties, free trials, etc. The best way to do this visually is under your CTA button. So, for this H fact business, underneath their CTA, you can see how they have these checkmarks around things like warranties and fast installations in conjunction with these badges. For our e-com client, we actually AB tested adding in these badges underneath the add to cart button, and we saw a 30% lift in conversions. For this SaaS company here, which is part of Alex Hormozi's portfolio, he features these three badges underneath the book demo button to reduce fear and uncertainty. So, the next part of Alex's value equation is time delay. Your copy needs to close the gap as much as possible as to how fast someone can get results. The best way to do this is through your headline and subheadline. So, for this online mediation course, the headline can be the dream outcome of building a six-figure mediation practice with the time aspect as well by saying less than 30 days. For this e-com supplement brand, they also highlight the time aspect by saying in just 2 weeks prominently in their headline. The final component of the value equation is reducing effort and sacrifice. If someone says, "Oh, you don't have to do anything" versus you have a ton of work associated with it, I'm more willing to pay for it. >> The first place that Alex recommends doing this is in the subheadline of your above the fold. The goal here with the subheadline is to basically explain how you achieve the result that you mentioned in your headline while also making it feel effortless and simple. >> So then the headline itself it's like we want it to be some sort of very clear like reason for them to move forward. And then I usually have sub headlines that clarify or add context to the headline. And so often times that'll be like without X, Y, and Z or without pain it qualifies it. >> So for example here the headline for this education business is launch a high-paying career or business fully debt free. And then the sub headline reduces the effort and sacrifice associated with achieving that result by talking about the fact that you get a personal success coach, you get 24/7 tutoring, hands-on skill training, and lifetime career support. Below the fold you can reduce effort and sacrifice through the how it works section where you basically explain all of the logistics of your product or service in, you know, three or four simple steps. Not five, not six, just three or four because the moment you go higher than three or four it increases the perceived effort.

**Для Напарника:** Под CTA: «бесплатно, без регистрации, данные хранятся только у тебя»; блок «как это работает» — ровно 3 шага.

## 6. Заголовки секций должны нести смысл сами: люди сканируют, а не читают
`лендинг · тексты · внимание` · [10:47](https://youtu.be/zA0B-VwOPn4?t=647)

**Мысль:** Представь, что читают только заголовки; вместо «как это работает» напиши суть («три шага — и остров растёт»).

**Слова автора:**

> The next part of Hermozi's strategy is focusing heavily on headlines throughout your entire landing page. Headlines come first for a reason. And the reason I have it as the first one is that it is the first and most important. So to quote David Ogilvy, once you've written your headline you've spent 80 cents of your advertising dollar, right? And so that means that the vast majority of people will only see the headlines in whatever it is that you make. It almost feels unfair that a headline is just a phrase or one word or one question, but it is also the thing that is ultimately going to get the most people to or have the biggest lever on whether your advertising is doing well. Statistically most people don't read, they scan. The mistake most people make is that they make their headlines very generic like, you know, here's what we do or how it works. You want to operate under the framework that somebody will only read the headlines on your page alone and just imagine they don't even read any of the body text. Is the value prop clear in just the headline alone? So instead of saying what makes this unique, say what actually makes you unique in just the headline itself. Instead of saying how it works, simply just explain how it works and how simple it is in the headline itself. Instead of what our customers say, simply just say what your customers actually say and the result in the headline itself.

**Для Напарника:** Заголовки секций лендинга и подписи на экранах формулировать как смысл, а не как рубрику.

## 7. Один сплит-тест в неделю; при малом трафике — крупные изменения по лучшим практикам
`метрики · процесс · конверсия` · [12:05](https://youtu.be/zA0B-VwOPn4?t=725)

**Мысль:** Фокус на одном тесте заставляет выбрать самое значимое; A/B требует 40–50 тыс. посетителей в месяц, иначе нет статзначимости.

**Слова автора:**

> Completely over complicate it, right? They think of hundreds of different things that they can test and they never end up knowing where to start. Alex's rule is super simple. It's just test one thing per week. The process is simple. Every week we have our marketing meeting and we do one split test. Just one. All right? And that's because everyone wants to test 100 things. But what it does is it forces us to be very focused on the one thing that we think is going to drive the highest result for us. Now you have to understand that this depends heavily on your traffic. Presumably somebody like Alex is driving, you know, tens of thousands if not hundreds of thousands of visitors to his landing pages across his portfolio every single month. He actually has sufficient traffic levels in order to do AB testing. Typically our minimum is at least 40 to 50,000 visitors per month. But let's say you only have 10,000 or 20,000 visitors per month or even lower than that. You're not going to be able to run traditional AB tests because you won't be able to reach statistical significance, which is kind of like a fancy way of saying is the test you did chance or not. So what we personally do for clients with lower traffic levels is we will make larger scale changes like completely revamping their landing page based on best practices on things that we've already AB tested with our higher traffic clients that get, you know, hundreds of thousands of visitors to millions of visitors. This is all in the absence of not having sufficient data to actually AB test.

**Для Напарника:** У маленького Напарника нет трафика для A/B: делать крупные обоснованные изменения и проверять на живых людях, а не на метриках.
