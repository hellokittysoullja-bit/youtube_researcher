## 1. Выписать все вопросы и найти ключевые «поворотные»
`процесс · исследования` · [00:22](https://youtu.be/kMWdmnQtJu0?t=22)

**Мысль:** Для сложной проблемы перечисляют все вопросы, большие и маленькие, и ищут те, ответ на которые снимает остальные.

**Слова автора:**

> Approach like a big open-ended problem is I sort of like just like write down all the questions that we have and all the questions that we need to solve and I actually remember doing this with fig Jam we had this messy page in our figma file that just listed like all of the different questions that we had and that we knew we would have to solve before launching this spatial writing has pretty quickly become my favorite way to think especially when dealing with really complex design challenges the meteor the problem the more likely I am to start my design process in fig Jam it's big questions like what is the relationship between figma and fig jam and small questions like how do you manipulate text like really being exhaustive there your goal is to figure out which questions do you have to answer that unlock answers to other questions as well I will go through and sort of look at the questions and say like what are the questions here that really feel like once we crack them all the answers to all the questions other questions will sort of be easy to answer and very obvious these Tipping Point questions are often referred to as igen questions and they're a key way to frame problems more effectively.

## 2. Показывать варианты на спектре: сначала крайности, потом середина
`процесс · дизайн-система` · [02:19](https://youtu.be/kMWdmnQtJu0?t=139)

**Мысль:** Вместо шести разрозненных макетов показывают два-три направления на шкале (например, насколько выразительным быть: от утилитарного до чрезмерного); крайности помогают увидеть, что даёт оптимизация одного свойства.

**Слова автора:**

> Now here's the mistake I see designers make too often let's say you're exploring one of these Tipping Point questions and you narrow in on like six different concepts that you think show a lot of potential now the last thing that you want to do is present these as six separate Concepts to the team here's Alex Cornell explaining why if you're just sharing a like a whole bunch of work like what do you guys think of this and you don't really make it clear what you were really toiling over you're going to get feedback all over the place so I think it's very important to establish and make very clear the framework that was guiding you when you did the thing so that it can sort of create an architecture around the ideas that you show that is very clear to people so that it's not like just getting a whole bunch of mocks thrown at you actually help them understand what is distinct about this idea why is it even being included in this combination and where does it sit on this little map that I'm going to draw for you that's why I'm such a big fan of presenting my work on a spectrum if we do a good job of identifying those Tipping Point questions then you can anchor all of your Explorations along that Spectrum now all of a sudden instead of looking at six isolated mockups you're presenting two or three highlevel directions that your team can take and the key is to paint the edges first and that's exactly what Jenny did while working on fig Jam like there's just two extremes so for example how fun and expressive should fig Jam be and you know there could be one side that's like super not expressive very utilitarian and one that's like over the top and I usually think with most design questions it's actually never the polar opposite it's always like somewhere in between the fine line is like figuring out where it is in that middle but I think just pushing the extremes lets you see like hey if we just optimize for one certain thing what does this actually design Direction look like when I'm presenting my ideas at crit or maybe just making a simple loom video to drop it in slack I'll often start my presentation with a simple visual representation of my Spectrum now this helps people understand my mental models before we dive into specific Concepts.

**Для Напарника:** При выборе тона и плотности эффектов (насколько живым делать Мир) двигаться по шкале «спокойно ↔ ярко», а не выбирать единичный вариант.

## 3. Приходить с мнением: чёткая позиция вынуждает реакцию
`процесс` · [05:08](https://youtu.be/kMWdmnQtJu0?t=308)

**Мысль:** В Instagram не давали переложить решение на других: если нет мнения, просят вернуться, когда оно появится. Ясная позиция заставляет людей реагировать.

**Слова автора:**

> The importance of having an opinion when presenting your work has definitely been the theme over the last 80 or so episodes Rich Arnold even said it was one of the main lessons that he learned after he left Vine and moved over to Instagram I think I had this habit of just sort of coming in with like a menu of solutions and being like what do you think yeah you want to pick one and at Instagram in general at Facebook like they're very good at not letting you take that decision off of your plate and put it on to them so if you come in and you don't have an opinion on what's right they'll just tell you to come back when you think you know what the right thing to do is and you have to come back and present why this is the right thing to do and the reason is when you have a clear point of view it forces people to react.

**Для Напарника:** Согласуется с принципом «Default Effect»: продукт сам предлагает вариант (25 минут, рекомендуем), а не выдаёт меню.

## 4. «Это может выглядеть примерно так» и высокая точность как фонарь
`процесс · крафт` · [09:35](https://youtu.be/kMWdmnQtJu0?t=575)

**Мысль:** Оговорка о возможности снимает придирки к пикселям; при этом реалистичные прототипы дают лучший отзыв, потому что вещь выглядит и ощущается настоящей.

**Слова автора:**

> Have always been good at telling an inspiring story showing what's possible but not showing what will be only showing what could be that's why when I'm exploring early Concepts the phrase that I use the most is it could look something like this that tiny little disclaimer is a powerful communication tool and a great way to avoid visual nitpicking now I know what you're thinking wouldn't it be more obvious if the designs were low Fidelity and possibly but again you get better feedback when something looks and feels real when you're exploring a cave High Fidelity prototypes serve as the brightest flashlights.
