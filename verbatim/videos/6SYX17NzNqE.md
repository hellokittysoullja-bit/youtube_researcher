## 1. Цепочка действий нарастает эмоционально
`игровой-дизайн · мотивация · нейро` · [02:56](https://youtu.be/6SYX17NzNqE?t=176)

**Мысль:** В комбо каждое успешное действие повышает ставки следующего; ошибка на четвёртом-пятом шаге ощущается сильнее, чем на первом. У цепочки есть эмоциональная дуга и нарастающая вовлечённость.

**Слова автора:**

> I think chains in games are interesting because of how they map on to emotion. And the most simple chain that I can think of that most people would probably think of if you said a chain of actions in a game would be like executing a combo in a fighting game. >> When you commit to doing a specific set or string of actions in a particular order, what happens is every one that you successfully complete raises the stakes about what's coming next. If you're trying to complete five actions in a row and you get the first or second one wrong, you're going to feel a bit bummed out. Oh no. When you're getting wrong on the fourth or fifth one, that is when it starts to feel like an epic fail. Oh, I almost had it. There is a emotional arc to a chain. You're not button bashing. You're trying to hit a very specific list of things in a specific order. It creates this rising sense of emotional investment with each thing you do.

**Для Напарника:** Очередь шагов (Zeigarnik) использует эту механику; осторожно: обрыв поздней цепочки больнее, поэтому нужны мягкие реплики кота при срыве.

## 2. Ценность цепочек: сбор ресурса значим из-за цели
`игровой-дизайн · мотивация · награды` · [04:03](https://youtu.be/6SYX17NzNqE?t=243)

**Мысль:** Подбор палок сам по себе не весел; смысл ему даёт то, что игрок планирует с ними сделать и как это служит эмоциональной фантазии игры (Dan Cook).

**Слова автора:**

> There is an essay by game designer Dan Cook. I will link it in the description. You should go and read it. It is about value chains. If you've got a economic chain in a game, like you collect these things and you use them for this. It's the what you use them for that gives the collection meaning and makes you feel something about it. So the example that Dan gives is picking up sticks is not inherently fun. You can make it as fun as you can. You can make it feel really toyike and they could be really sparkly sticks or whatever, but it's not an inherently fun activity. It's not enough to carry a game. What makes the activity enjoyable is what the player plans to do with the sticks and how that fulfills the like core fantasy of the game, the core emotional thing that the game is pulling at.

**Для Напарника:** Сессия Фокуса значима, когда видна цель: монетки или энергия идут на конкретное развитие острова.

## 3. Конечная цель делает смысловым каждое действие в пути
`игровой-дизайн · мотивация · награды` · [04:55](https://youtu.be/6SYX17NzNqE?t=295)

**Мысль:** В Fallout 4 строительство превратило хлам в осмысленные ресурсы, и решения «что поднять» стали богаче. Понимание цели делает сбор интереснее.

**Слова автора:**

> Another really good example of this would be there was a little bit of crafting in Fallout 3 and then in Fallout New Vegas there was a little bit more crafting and then in Fallout 4 you're now like the mayor of Boston and you need to rebuild Boston using twigs and super glue. There are people that don't like those base building mechanics. quite possible to play the game while ignoring those mechanics. But my experience of it was personally being able to build stuff out of the junk in the game changed everything. It meant that objects that previously were just a piece of junk to be collected and sold and maybe you would think a little bit about like the cost to weight ratio of different objects as you picked them up, they were now imbued with a different sort of meaning because now I'm trying to build the lighting for my 1950s Hawaiian Aloha bar and I need to look out for items that have copper wiring in them. So, I'm learning which fans and telephones have copper wiring in them. the destination of the chain that we know we're working towards changes the feel of engaging with mechanics earlier on because knowing that I'm trying to rebuild Boston with twigs and glue means that every time I go into a place and I'm looting it, the decision space of what do I pick up, what do I leave behind is suddenly richer because I'm not just taking every gun to sell at the merchant because that has the best weight to cost ratio or something like that. having a kind of forward destination for these resources I'm collecting. The action of collecting the stuff becomes more meaningful and interesting and exciting to do because the player understands what they are trying to achieve by doing it.

**Для Напарника:** Показывать, к какой постройке ведёт текущий шаг или сессия, чтобы малые действия получали смысл.

## 4. Скрытые цепочки секретов: игрок чувствует себя умным
`игровой-дизайн · дофамин · награды` · [06:36](https://youtu.be/6SYX17NzNqE?t=396)

**Мысль:** Секретные цепочки (фейри, скрытая концовка) дают открытие, когда человек проходит мимо десятки раз и затем собирает картину. Особенно работают в играх с повторными забегами.

**Слова автора:**

> Spelunky has a chain in it of interesting secrets that lead up to kind of the proper ending of the game. It's not the only game that's done this. Castlevania Symphony of the Night had a similar thing. Basically, in both games, you can do what you think is completing the game. And then there's a hidden better way of completing the game. In order to do it, you'd have to stumble across this chain of secrets or you'd have to read about it online or something like that. I think that's cool. That's a chain where the player doesn't really know it's a chain. And I've been trying to incorporate some of these into the game that I'm currently working on. There are these fairies. They're reasonably trivial to spawn, but the first few times the player manages to spawn these fairies, they're not actually going to know what triggered it unless they read it on the internet. They won't really know how they did it. And the fairies will be part of a chain where if the player, and by doing that, they'll have unlocked that and then they'll feel really good about themselves. But they'll probably encounter the fairies quite a few times before they realize that they're part of a chain. and that there is a very simple way to start that chain and to spawn the fairies. They just they'll be doing it by accident to start off with. I have a similar plan for this horrific mouth monster. This is going to be the start of a chain. And if you feed it a I think this type of design is interesting in a systemic game like a rogike because the player is doing lots and lots of runs of doing the same thing and that vastly increases their chance of just stumbling across the solution to one of these things. It's kind of how the chains work in Spalunky. It's how some of the puzzle chains work in blueprints. I really, really like that feeling of you walk past something dozens of times before you realize that it's significant and it's been under your nose all this time and then you put the pieces together and there's something really pleasing about that. And I love the depth that can add to a game which has relatively simple controls.

**Для Напарника:** Спрятать на острове редкие находки, зависящие от регулярного присутствия, и открывать их без подсказки, как «заслуженное» дофаминовое открытие.

## 5. Два вида цепочек: явная цель и скрытое открытие
`игровой-дизайн · мотивация` · [09:09](https://youtu.be/6SYX17NzNqE?t=549)

**Мысль:** Явные цепочки определяются мотивацией построить нечто; скрытые дают чувство «ага, я сам догадался».

**Слова автора:**

> You can get clear chains where players know where they're going and the feeling of the chain is defined by the player's motivation to build a particular thing. Or you can get these weird secret chains where the player feels smart because aha now I realized that I was actually making the fairies spawn by.

**Для Напарника:** Сочетать явные цели (следующий ориентир) и тихие скрытые находки на острове.
