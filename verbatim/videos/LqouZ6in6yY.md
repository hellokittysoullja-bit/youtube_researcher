## 1. Культура хаслa - как джанк-фуд для СДВГ-мозга
`дофамин · мотивация · этика` · [01:17](https://youtu.be/LqouZ6in6yY?t=77)

**Мысль:** Быстрый дофаминовый всплеск от воображаемой работы и последующий провал; мозгу нужен всё более сильный стимул.

**Слова автора:**

> The self-help and motivational industry have been incentivized to lie to you. They sold you the idea that the secret to success is to hustle harder, but hustle culture is like junk food for ADHD brains. Fast dopamine spike, faster crash, and over time your brain needs more extreme stimulus just to feel normal. You get the dopamine hit from imagining yourself doing the work, but when you sit at your desk, nothing happens.

**Для Напарника:** Не строить на мотивирующих цитатах и «хайпе»; награды за реальное действие, а не за намерение.

## 2. Интерес-ориентированная нервная система: активация приходит снаружи
`СДВГ · мотивация · нейро · исследования` · [01:50](https://youtu.be/LqouZ6in6yY?t=110)

**Мысль:** Мозг СДВГ включается интересом, вызовом, новизной и срочностью, а не важностью; нехватка внутреннего топлива делает силу воли бесполезной.

**Слова автора:**

> Dr. William Dodson, one of the world's leading ADHD researchers, spent years studying why standard productivity and self-help advice consistently failed people with ADHD. What he found changed how we understand the condition entirely. He calls it the interest-based nervous system. Neurotypical brains activate for importance, for deadlines, for long-term reward. Our brains activate for one thing, interest. And here's why that matters, because a peer-reviewed study published in Molecular Psychiatry confirmed that ADHD brains have a structural deficit in the dopamine reward pathway, the circuit responsible for generating internal motivation. Which means willpower was never going to work, not because you're weak, but because you are missing the fuel at a neurological level for it to work. What Dr. Dodson found is that every single thing that activates our nervous system comes from outside of us. Challenge, novelty, urgency, they're all different triggers in the same category known as external.

**Для Напарника:** Дизайн-принцип: создавать внешние условия активации (вызов, новизна, срочность) прямо в Фокусе, а не требовать воли.

## 3. Несколько финишей: короткие окна с видимым финишем и частые завершения
`СДВГ · время · награды · дофамин` · [03:22](https://youtu.be/LqouZ6in6yY?t=202)

**Мысль:** Дедлайн «через 2 недели» нейрологически не существует; короткое окно с финишной чертой даёт сигнал завершения и микро-дофамин; проектируй для частых завершений, а не долгих сессий.

**Слова автора:**

> Multiple finish lines. Our brains don't respond to deadlines 2 weeks from now. The deadline doesn't neurologically exist until it's about 18 hours away. And by then, the anxiety is doing all the work, not us. But here's what does work, breaking your day into a series of short windows, each with its own finish line. Not finish the project by Friday, that's one finish line. Our brains tune out. Instead, work on this for 50 minutes, then stop. That's a finish line your brain can actually see, and a brain that can see the finish line will run toward it. The key insight from Dr. Ned Hallowell, who spent 30 years treating ADHD, is that ADHDers don't lack motivation globally. We lack the ability to sustain activation over a long, open-ended stretches of time. Shrink the window and the activation problem mostly disappears. Here's why that works neurologically. Every time you cross a finish line, even a small one, your brain registers a completion signal, a micro reward, a small hit of dopamine that confirms you did the thing. Now you get the feeling. Neurotypical brains can defer that signal for hours. We can't. We need it more frequently, not because we're impatient, but because our dopamine pathway requires more regular input to stay activated. So, you're not designing for long work sessions, you're designing for frequent completions.

**Для Напарника:** Подтверждение коротких сессий Фокуса с чётким финишем и тёплым закрытием каждой; дробить день на окна.

## 4. Ферраари с велосипедными тормозами: между окнами нужно движение, а не залипание
`СДВГ · дофамин · внимание · хаптика` · [05:24](https://youtu.be/LqouZ6in6yY?t=324)

**Мысль:** Движение (прогулка, смена комнаты) между окнами сбрасывает истощение и запускает следующее окно с активации.

**Слова автора:**

> But while your ADHD brain is definitely a Ferrari when it has an end point, you have bicycle brakes, which is why what you do in between the windows matters as much as the windows themselves. Movement is one of the most underused ADHD productivity tools. A walk, a workout, even just changing rooms, pausing doesn't have to be boring. You don't have to just sit there. Movement triggers a dopamine release, which means by the time you sit back down for the next window, your brain isn't depleted. It's reset. The structure looks like this. Focus work window, movement or reset, focused work window. You're not just collecting finish lines. You're cycling dopamine deliberately, so each window starts with activation instead of dragging the fatigue of the last one into it.

**Для Напарника:** После сессии предложить мини-перерыв с движением («пройдись, кот подождёт у костра») вместо следующего скролла.

## 5. Эффект со-действия: присутствие другого сильнее усиливает активацию при СДВГ
`СДВГ · body-doubling · исследования · нейро` · [06:35](https://youtu.be/LqouZ6in6yY?t=395)

**Мысль:** Исследования Триплетта и новая работа по body doubling: рядом работающий человек синхронизирует активацию.

**Слова автора:**

> There's a principle in neuroscience called the co-action effect. First documented by Norman Triplett at Indiana University over a hundred years ago, and repeatedly confirmed since, it describes something simple. People perform better on tasks when other people are present doing the same thing. For neurotypical people, this is a modest effect. For ADHD brains, it's significantly stronger. A new research article investigating body doubling with neurodivergent participants found that if you have ADHD, seeing other people focus may actually prime our own brains to want to do the same. When you sit down to work next to someone who's also working, you're not just sharing a room. Your brain is co-regulating with theirs, borrowing their activation state.

**Для Напарника:** Обоснование присутствия кота-напарника и возможной опции «сидим вместе» с другими людьми.

## 6. Конкретная подотчётность: кто, когда, что, на сколько
`СДВГ · мотивация · body-doubling · процесс` · [11:29](https://youtu.be/LqouZ6in6yY?t=689)

**Мысль:** Расплывчатое «поработаю сегодня» слабо; чёткие время, активность и длительность с другими создают социальное обязательство и идентичность «я из тех, кто приходит».

**Слова автора:**

> Make accountability specific. This is the one most people miss. Let's say you're looking for a job in the age of AI. Telling someone I'm going to work on this today doesn't do much even if you're sitting in that cafe. Motivation may fade because your ADHD brain doesn't just run on intention, it runs on identity. We follow through on what people like us are doing. When you're working with random people at random times, there is no signal for who you are in that moment. But the moment you say I'll be on a call at 2:00 p.m. with other people who are also submitting job applications and I'll be on that call until 2:50 p.m., something fundamental happens. You've just made the time and activity you're working on specific. Specific accountability has four elements your brain can actually process. Who, when, what, and for how long.

**Для Напарника:** Старт сессии как маленькое обещание напарнику: что и на сколько минут; кот «ждёт к 15:00».

## 7. В режиме угрозы мозг не учится и не стартует: сначала безопасность
`СДВГ · стыд · нейро · доверие` · [14:18](https://youtu.be/LqouZ6in6yY?t=858)

**Мысль:** Накопленные доказательства «я проблема» включают реакцию угрозы; никакая система не работает, пока среда не кажется безопасной.

**Слова автора:**

> Most of us show up to work on a task already carrying evidence. Evidence that we are the problem. That we can't follow through. That we've failed at this before. That weight doesn't just affect our confidence. It activates the threat response. The part of the brain that's scanning for danger. And when the brain is in threat mode, it doesn't learn. It doesn't try new things. It doesn't initiate. It protects. Which means no tool, system, or accountability method works until the environment itself feels safe. The path isn't try harder and get results. It's feel safe, try things, learn from them, trust yourself, and repeat.

**Для Напарника:** Главное обоснование безопасного, тёплого тона без стыда во всём продукте; безопасность важнее механик.
