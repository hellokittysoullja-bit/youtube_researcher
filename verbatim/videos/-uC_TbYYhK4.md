## 3. Эксперимент с фотографами: количество приводит к качеству
`процесс · мотивация` · [06:11](https://youtu.be/-uC_TbYYhK4?t=371)

**Мысль:** Группу, оцениваемую по числу снимков, результат получился лучше, чем у группы, оцениваемой по одному идеальному: те много теоретизировали и получили один посредственный кадр.

**Слова автора:**

> Gabriel Valdivia, who helps early stage companies, provided a helpful approach which he believes can benefit organizations of any scale. His core directive progress, not perfection, is the engine of innovation. Maybe not a huge surprise, but the insight came not from valuing quality over quantity, but understanding that quantity is often how we get to quality. He gave the example of an experiment conducted by Jerry Uelsmann, a photographer and teacher who split his students into two groups for an activity. You may have heard of this one before. Group A would be graded based on the total quality of the photos that they took, whilst Group B only had to produce a single photo and that alone would be based on quality. Group A, who were tasked with taking as many photos as possible. Naturally spent more time in the practice, which yielded far greater results than the team asked to focus on quality. They spent too much time theorizing and were left with a single mediocre shot.

**Для Напарника:** Близко к принципу Напарника: маленькие шаги и много коротких стартов важнее одного идеального; можно использовать в текстах про прощение несовершенства.

## 4. Дешёвые одноразовые артефакты снижают эмоциональную привязанность
`ИИ · процесс · стыд` · [08:03](https://youtu.be/-uC_TbYYhK4?t=483)

**Мысль:** Когда идею легко выбросить, меньше привязанности и страха суждения; пустой холст превращается в площадку, где единственная ошибка это мало пробовать.

**Слова автора:**

> So AI can lower the cost of risk with tools like Figma Make, generating high fidelity prototypes from designs or even text prompts in minutes. This lowers the cost of experimentation. If a wild idea flops, you haven't lost days or weeks of effort. The speed and disposability of AI generated artifacts makes it easier to fail forward. It can also reduce emotional investment. If AI can help you spin up and discard ideas rapidly, you're less likely to become attached to a single solution. This detachment makes it easier to take creative risks and embrace the learning that comes from failure. And finally, it can also help democratize feedback by increasing the surface area in which feedback that feedback is applied. AI helps to visualize and share examples in real time, making it easier to invite feedback from a broader group. It's also more participatory in the early stage, with lower barriers to entry for non designers to experiment. As I'm sure many of us have experienced with developers and product managers getting involved in that vibe coding aspect. This supports Valdivia’s call to share before you're ready and builds collective ownership, reducing the fear of judgment. In short, AI can act as a creative amplifier and the safety nets, making the messy, iterative process of early stage design less intimidating and more playful. It's a way to turn the fear the blank canvas into playground of possibility, where the only real mistake is not trying enough things fast enough to discover what works.
