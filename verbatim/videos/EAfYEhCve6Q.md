## 1. Сегментируй по мотивации, а не по поведению; SDT как простая модель
`мотивация · исследования · СДВГ · удержание` · [01:42](https://youtu.be/EAfYEhCve6Q?t=102)

**Мысль:** Каждое действие в продукте либо закрывает потребность, либо нет; без потребности человек приходит только по необходимости. Простейшая модель SDT: автономия, компетентность, принадлежность.

**Слова автора:**

> User psychology. Product analytics usually splits users by their behavior. How often they visit? How did they go in the funnel? How much they spend. Game design splits users differently. It start with motivation. These are two very different approaches and they lead to very different product architectures. Here is the basic tool, a model that sorts players into groups based on what motivates them. There are similar models for products too. They point to a few core types of motivation. Your job as a product architect is to figure out how your product meets each one. Every action in your product either meets one of these needs or it meets none of them. If a feature doesn't give the user what they are after, they'll come back only when they really have to. They won't get pulled in. They definitely won't be happy around it. That means lower engagement, low retention, lower altitude. Gamified systems follow one simple rule. Give every type of user a way to meet their needs. Motivation should shape everything. Your features, your tone of voice, your UI UIX, even how your design, your offers. If you don't keep every user type in view, some of them will just drop off. Not because they're the wrong users, because your product has no depth for them, no triggers built for them. And that shows up as retention decrease. and exactly those segments. The easiest model to learn and to use is SDT, selfdetermination theory. A huge number of modern products already run on it. It names just three core motivations. Autonomy, competence, and social belonging. Find out how your users meet each of them in your product.

**Для Напарника:** Проверять каждый экран Напарника по SDT: даёт ли он автономию (выбор), компетентность (прогресс острова), принадлежность (кот рядом).

## 2. FTUE: быстрая первая победа и 3 уровня объяснений, ядро первым
`онбординг · активация · удержание · внимание` · [03:33](https://youtu.be/EAfYEhCve6Q?t=213)

**Мысль:** Окно от установки до первой пользы должно дать быстрый результат (aha). Не объясняй всё сразу: стена текста это когнитивная перегрузка; сначала ядро, остальное по мере необходимости.

**Слова автора:**

> FTE stands for first time user experience. It's the stretch of time between install and the first moment the user gets real value. In game design, this window has one job. Give the player a fast result. The key moment here is the aha moment when the player suddenly gets it gets how to play. A fast first win is a great lance for any hot product. It's usually the single biggest lever on day one retention. F2E is part of on boarding at the same time. Here is one of game designers biggest mistakes. They are trying to explain everything at once, trying to show everything at once. Non-game products do this too. Look at most SAS products for example, walls of text explaining every single button and menu. But a big dump of text up front is just cognitive overload. So when you explain what to do, you need to prioritize. First tire, second tire, third tire. The first tire is always the core loop that always comes first. Everything else gets introduced only when the user actually needs it. Onboarding always feels like homework. It never feels like part of the fun and that's exactly where most products lose the most users game or not. This is where you lose the most day one retention before LTV even gets a chance to build.

**Для Напарника:** Онбординг Напарника: первый шаг и таймер сразу, всё остальное (остров, ориентиры, имя) вводится позже и по одному.

## 3. Обучение внутри первого действия и вычеркни половину шагов
`онбординг · активация · запуск-задачи` · [05:04](https://youtu.be/EAfYEhCve6Q?t=304)

**Мысль:** Пользователи не читают инструкции до шага один: объясняй шаг два только после того, как шаг один сработал. Спрашивай, что можно вырезать из первых 30 секунд.

**Слова автора:**

> Shouldn't come before the action. It should happen inside the first useful action. Users don't read instructions before step one. They just take step one. Then the products explain step two. But only after step one is done and only after it's already paid off. That's how contextual learning works. Just in time on boarding. Second principle, cut every step you can on the way to first value. A game designer always asks, "What can I cut from the first 30 seconds without losing the point?" In most non-game products, the answer is cut half your current signup steps, half your profile setup, half your interface explanation. Every step you hear praises your activation rate and your day one retouching.

**Для Напарника:** Не просить регистрацию или настройку до первой сессии; профиль и имя предлагать после первой пользы.

## 4. Три вложенных цикла: сессия, прогрессия, мета; что происходит между сессиями
`удержание · привычки · игровой-дизайн · время` · [06:08](https://youtu.be/EAfYEhCve6Q?t=368)

**Мысль:** Ретеншн падает не потому, что продукт плох, а потому что нет второго и третьего циклов. Вопрос: что происходит, пока пользователя нет в приложении?

**Слова автора:**

> Engagement loops. The core loop is the simplest kind of engagement loop. It's the smallest step of actions a user repeats over and over. Action result, new reason to act again. In games, this gets designed first before anything else. Because DA and retention depend on it directly. Game design usually has at least three loops nested inside each other. The session loop what happens in one visit over a few minutes. The progression loop, what happens between visits over hours or days. usually about building something up. This is what drives your day seven and day 30 retention. The metal loop, the third one, long-term goals over weeks or months, usually bigger goals than any single action. This is what drives long-term LTV. Here is what a hot product could borrow. Don't stop at the session loop. Build more depth. Give the user a reason to come back tomorrow. That reason is exactly what shows up as retention on your dashboard. Dow and retention don't drop because your product is bad. They drop because the second and the third loops are missing and almost nobody will. Here is a good question for any product. What happens between sessions while your user is not in the app? If their answer is nothing, no buildup, no progress, nothing to look forward to, done the loop race at the very first level, no amount of push notifications, you'll get retention past occasional and LTV plateaus right along with it.

**Для Напарника:** Остров должен «жить» между сессиями (небо по часу суток, очаг, существо у костра) и давать повод вернуться завтра без пушей.

## 5. Прогрессия: награждай малые шаги, потом сложные; награды непредсказуемы
`награды · дофамин · геймификация · удержание` · [07:48](https://youtu.be/EAfYEhCve6Q?t=468)

**Мысль:** Прогрессия показывает сумму сделанного и ощущение роста. Начинай с наград за малые шаги; предсказуемая награда теряет дофамин, нужны переменные.

**Слова автора:**

> Every product needs progression. games, at tech, big tech, all of it. Progression is a system that shows the user the sum of everything they've done. A good one pulls the user deeper step by step. It hooks them. It builds a habit and it usually comes with strong retention and LTV. A sense of progression means one thing. The user can clearly see they're getting better at something. more control over their money, better health, better habits, sharper skills, whatever it is. To build this feeling, products lean on basic psychology, milestones, celebrating small wins, progress bars, streaks, like in Dualingo, for example, badges, levels, challenges, and more. And rewards, lock the filling in. And here is how rewards should work. There is a separate principle. First, reward every small step. opening a new section, finishing the first task, inviting a friend, filling out a profile, then move forwards to hard direction, one that take more effort over time. A reward you can't predict stops exciting you. It stops giving you that dopamine hit. So, you need variable rewards, randomizers, loot boxes with different odds, that kind of thing. The arrival rewards are the only kind that keeps triggering dopamine again and again every time and they keep moving retention and are pool every time.

**Для Напарника:** Награждать даже за начало; редкие находки на острове как переменная награда. Лутбоксы и азарт не применимы: только мягкая непредсказуемость.

## 6. Полезное трение: IKEA-эффект, но не в основном цикле
`крафт · мотивация · активация · запуск-задачи` · [09:46](https://youtu.be/EAfYEhCve6Q?t=586)

**Мысль:** Игры вводят трение намеренно: то, что строишь сам, ценнее. Но трение добавляют только перед бонусом, никогда в core loop; бесполезное трение (формы, навигация) убирают.

**Слова автора:**

> The last one, the fifth one is friction. Usually, friction is any effort your product asks from the user before they get a result. The standard product playbook says cut friction everywhere. Always game design does the opposite. It builds friction in on purpose. No effort, no reward and that means no dopamine hit from overcoming it. Some friction is just bad. For example, I don't know extra form fields, confusing navigation, typing the same thing twice. this kind your conversions, it cost you your day one retention. But some friction is designed on purpose to make the result feel more valuable for the user. Here is a classic example. The EK something you build yourself feels more valuable than the exact same thing handed to you finished completed. Even though it's objectively identical for hot products, here is my advice. Go carefully. First rule, only add friction in front of something extra, something added. A chunky UI for example is just friction with no PO that's a fast track to drop off was sessions was retention was DO but a temporary cash back boost for a bit of extra effort that's a real benefit I guess and the lever on our pool second rule never put friction in your core loop that kills your product it filters people out it ts retention but friction around a gift that's a different that's a chance for the user to invest something of their own in non-game products that this looks like personalizing your setup, building your own profile, building your rowing dashboard, any step where the user puts in a little effort because before they get the result, cutting that kind of friction is not always a win for retention or LTV. Sometimes it just makes the product feel less valuable. The real job isn't to remove all friction. It's to sort it into two piles and only clear out the kind that's useless.

**Для Напарника:** Личная настройка (имя, выбор шага, оформление) даёт чувство своего; но запуск сессии должен быть в один тап, без трения в ядре.
