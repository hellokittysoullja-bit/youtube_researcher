## 1. Белое пространство активно: паника перед пустотой, а воздух успокаивает
`отступы · внимание · СДВГ` · [00:32](https://youtu.be/FDFD2-poyGw?t=32)

**Мысль:** Новички заполняют пустоту, но добавление отступов делает дизайн спокойнее и увереннее без новых элементов.

**Слова автора:**

> White space isn't empty. It's actually an important and somewhat active part of a design. A huge problem early on is that new designers often panic when they see blank space on their designs. They feel the urge to fill it with something or anything. And let's face it, some beginner clients do this, too. But try this. Take a busy flyer or poster and then add generous padding around the text blocks, space between the sections, and breathing room around the logo. Suddenly, everything calms down. Your eyes feel like they can finally relax when looking at this design. This is your canvas learning how to breathe. It creates balance, structure, and confidence. And without even adding a new element, the design looks 10 times better.

**Для Напарника:** Оставлять воздух на Доме и Фокусе; пустота это не минус, а ноль, и совпадает с тоном продукта.

## 2. Группировка близостью и проверка прищуриванием
`отступы · иерархия · крафт` · [01:26](https://youtu.be/FDFD2-poyGw?t=86)

**Мысль:** Близкие элементы воспринимаются как группа. Если прищуриться, структура должна читаться по одному размещению пробелов, ещё до чтения текста.

**Слова автора:**

> In this stage, we use whites space to create groups or sections. This is where things like the Gestto principles come into play, especially proximity. Elements that are close together are seen as a group. Elements that are spaced out are seen as separate. So on your layouts, you might try grouping a heading with a subheading and then giving a bit of space for the body text below. Think of it like a table seating area. People sitting close are probably talking to each other. And when you apply this visually, it becomes a conversation between design elements, and the viewer instantly understands how your content is structured. To sharpen this even more, try this. squint your eyes and look at your layout in a blurry way. You should still be able to tell which elements belong together just by how the white space shapes the page. That's the power of grouping or proximity. It should work even before you read a single word.

**Для Напарника:** Каждый экран проверять блюром или прищуриванием: видны ли группы без чтения.

## 3. Пространство направляет внимание без стрелок и цвета
`внимание · иерархия · отступы` · [02:47](https://youtu.be/FDFD2-poyGw?t=167)

**Мысль:** Дать кнопке больше воздуха значит привлечь к ней взгляд; уменьшить зазор значит связать заголовок с абзацем. Слабая иерархия заставляет пользователя работать.

**Слова автора:**

> It can direct focus without using arrows, colors, or extra graphics. Let's say you have a heading, a paragraph, and a button on a UI. By adjusting the spacing between them, you can decide what gets noticed first. And if you want to highlight that button, you might want to give it some generous breathing room. And if you want to tie the headings closely to the paragraph, just reduce that gap. A design with poor hierarchy forces the user to work extra effort on your design. And a design with smart white space guides them naturally. You're leading the eye like a dance partner just by using spacing.

**Для Напарника:** Главную кнопку старта окружить заметно большим воздухом, чем остальные элементы Фокуса.

## 4. Микро- и макро-пространство и единая пространственная шкала
`отступы · сетка · дизайн-система` · [03:40](https://youtu.be/FDFD2-poyGw?t=220)

**Мысль:** Микро это интерлиньяж и padding, макро поля и интервалы между секциями. Единая шкала значений (4, 8, 32) на обоих уровнях создаёт ритм, доверие и целостность.

**Слова автора:**

> Known as micro white space. Whereas the larger areas of white space on a design between groups and large areas is called macro whites space. Which leads us on to stage four in this video on white space in graphic design. This is where you start seeing white space as an actual full-bodied system. So there's micro white space, the small scale spacing like letter spacing, line heights, padding inside components and so on. And then we have macro white space. So the margins, the gutters, the space between large sections, but both matter. I'm soon going to see why. But what actually works really well on a design is using a spatial scale. It's a consistent system of values. So maybe 4 8632 and apply them across both micro and macro layers. This creates rhythm, trust, and cohesion even if you allow it is complex. So you might have four point micro space between your body text lines, but then eight points between the heading and that body text and then also 32 from the entire text group to the design image or asset to the right. Micro white space is more about helping the viewer feel comfortable in obtaining information from text and being able to navigate finer details on a design.

**Для Напарника:** Сформулировать в токенах Напарника микро (4–8) и макро (24–32) шаги и не смешивать их.

## 5. Пространство передаёт эмоцию: премиальность, энергия, спокойствие
`отступы · вкус · крафт` · [06:36](https://youtu.be/FDFD2-poyGw?t=396)

**Мысль:** Apple использует много воздуха для премиальности, панк-афиша тесноту для энергии. Отступы это инструмент настроения.

**Слова автора:**

> Every space you add or subtract says something. Apple uses a lot of white space because it says premium, modern, minimal. A punk band flyer might use tight, chaotic spacing because it says energy, non-conformist, and rule. Whites space can speak volumes without saying a single word or displaying a single word. So ask yourself, how do I want this brand or this design to feel? Is it clean and calm, energetic and raw, playful and quirky? Then use space to build that feeling on your design. And this is where you're not just designing visuals, you're designing moods, you're designing for emotion.

**Для Напарника:** Для тёплого прощающего тона Напарника использовать щедрый воздух и мягкие скругления.

## 6. Темп через воздух: плотные блоки быстрые, широкие паузы замедляют
`отступы · внимание · анимация` · [09:06](https://youtu.be/FDFD2-poyGw?t=546)

**Мысль:** Чередование плотных и свободных участков держит внимание живым, а неожиданные паузы заставляют что-то почувствовать.

**Слова автора:**

> You can think of your layout like a piece of music. Some beats are fast and dense, others are slow and almost quiet. The alternation builds rhythm. And rhythm keeps attention alive. And you can use spatial pacing in this way. So vary the intervals of white space to control how quickly or how slowly the eye moves. The standard rule of thumb is tight spacing equals fast urgent beats. Wide spacing is slow dramatic pauses and unexpected gaps interrupt expectation making your viewer feel something. And for example, imagine a vertical scroll experience. Instead of uniform sections, you might add breathing space between some blocks and then suddenly tighten things up. That contrast right there creates a rhythm.

**Для Напарника:** В длинной ленте чата и на лендинге чередовать плотные и свободные блоки, а паузу ставить перед наградой.
