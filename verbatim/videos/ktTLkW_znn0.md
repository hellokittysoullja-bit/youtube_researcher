## 1. Слепота ко времени: прошлое ушло, будущее туман, настоящее — всё
`время · СДВГ · стыд` · [00:56](https://youtu.be/ktTLkW_znn0?t=56)

**Мысль:** Это не про усилия, а про доступ: нет внутреннего чувства времени, поэтому человек работает вдвое тяжелее и всё равно отстаёт; стыд вторичен.

**Слова автора:**

> ADHD brain's inability to accurately perceive or feel time. It's not that we don't know what time it is. I mean, we can read a clock. It's just that we don't have an internal sense of it the way neurotypical people do. It shows up in three big ways. The past feels like it's gone forever. Like once something is done, it's out of sight, out of mind. Learning from past mistakes or building habits takes extra effort. And the future is foggy. You can intend to do something next week, but future you feels completely disconnected from now you. That's why preparing early feels almost impossible. The present is everything. ADHD brains tend to live in two times now and not now. If something is not happening right this second, it might as well not exist. This is neurobiological. It's tied to how the ADHD brain processes time through the prefrontal cortex, the part of your brain responsible for planning, sequencing, and forward thinking. And because our brains don't naturally track time passing, like the movement of the sun, like light, or I don't know, hunger cues when we're hyperfocused, we miss cues, like I just said, that others don't. So, here's where this starts to get emotional. Time blindness doesn't just cause inconvenience. It often causes shame. You're late to a meeting or forget an appointment again. You lose track of time and let someone down. You start the day with great intention and it ends up feeling like you failed. It's so easy to internalize that to tell yourself, "I just need to be more disciplined or worse, what's wrong with me?" But the truth is, it isn't about effort. It's about access. When your brain literally can't perceive time in a reliable way, you end up working twice as hard just to stay on time. And even then, you're still behind.

**Для Напарника:** Показывать время на экране Фокуса наглядно (кольцо/остров), без обвинений за опоздание или незапуск.

## 2. Нервная система основана на интересе, не на приоритете
`СДВГ · дофамин · мотивация` · [03:32](https://youtu.be/ktTLkW_znn0?t=212)

**Мысль:** Важное, но скучное годами не делается, а срочное и эмоциональное делается сразу; «ложь про время» — попытка мозга добыть дофамин.

**Слова автора:**

> ADHD timelines we tell ourselves. This will only take 5 minutes. It doesn't. I've got plenty of time. You don't. I'll do it later. You won't. I work better under pressure. Okay, maybe. But do you feel better when you do it that way? Often times, you're disregulating your nervous system, which doesn't really benefit you in the long run. These lies aren't a sign that we're delusional. They're your brain doing its best to create dopamine where there isn't any or limited access. Because here's the truth. ADHD brains don't respond to importance. They respond to interest. That's why things that are urgent, exciting, or emotionally charged get done fast. And boring but important tasks sit untouched for weeks. And I say weeks, but maybe years, right? We have interestbased nervous systems, not priority based ones.

**Для Напарника:** Делать скучный шаг интересным: игровая обёртка, мини-награда после старта, а не рассказ о важности.

## 3. Пять приёмов: время наружу, контейнеры дня, микро-дедлайны с «зачем», игра
`время · запуск-задачи · геймификация` · [04:41](https://youtu.be/ktTLkW_znn0?t=281)

**Мысль:** Визуальный таймер, контейнеры «после обеда» вместо точных минут, мини-дедлайн с причиной, гонка со временем и дофаминовый сэндвич.

**Слова автора:**

> Number one, externalize time. You can't feel time passing, so make it visible. Use analon clocks so you can see time move. Get a time timer or similar visual countdown timer. Create a visual schedule using whiteboards, sticky notes, or block calendars. Number two, chunk your day into time containers. Instead of saying, "I'll do this today," say, "I'll work on this in the afternoon," or, "I'll work on this after lunch." This helps your brain hold the shape of the day without micromanaging every minute, which then can end up feel like a cage. Number three, use micro deadlines with context. Don't just set a big deadline. Set mini check-in points and always include why it matters. email slides today by 3 PM so I'm not scrambling before the meeting. Number four, gamify the task. ADHD brains love novelty and challenge, so race the clock to see how fast you can get it done. Use Focusmate or another body doubling tool. Try a dopamine sandwich by doing something fun, then the boringish task, then reward yourself again.

**Для Напарника:** Таймер-кольцо и «после обеда»-подсказки на Доме; награда сразу после скучного шага.

## 4. Сначала регуляция, потом действие
`СДВГ · запуск-задачи · хаптика` · [05:48](https://youtu.be/ktTLkW_znn0?t=348)

**Мысль:** В режиме бей/беги/замри мозг не планирует; помогают дыхание, тёплое/мягкое касание, 5-4-3-2-1; пересилить старт без регуляции не выходит.

**Слова автора:**

> Number five, regulate before you act. If your nervous system is in fight, flight, freeze or shutdown, you will not be able to plan or start things because the only thing your brain body are concerned about that time or focused on is survival. So before you reach for your to-do list, check in with your body. Are you grounded? Do you feel safe enough to begin? And I'm going to be real honest. This morning I was like, h not I'm not regulated. I just got to I just got to start. I got to start. And you know what? After about 10 minutes, I was so unfocused and scattered. I thought to myself, "This isn't working. I need to follow my own advice and do something to regulate my nervous system." And I have other videos that goes over that more, but for now, try doing something like noticing your natural breath and inviting it to slow, maybe even deepen into your belly as you notice it. Maybe touch something soft or warm. Do a little movement or practice 5 4 3 2 1 by noticing five things you see, four things you feel to the touch, three things you hear, two things you smell, and one thing you taste. Regulation opens the door to executive function.

**Для Напарника:** Перед сессией предложить 3 медленных вдоха с котом как необязательный мягкий ритуал входа.

## 5. Опоздания — не провалы, а сигнал, что нужна система и сострадание
`СДВГ · стыд · этика` · [07:06](https://youtu.be/ktTLkW_znn0?t=426)

**Мысль:** Успех со временем — снижение хаоса, защита энергии и доверие своим ритмам, а не идеальная пунктуальность.

**Слова автора:**

> Here's the big shift I want you to walk away with. You don't need to be a time management wizard. You don't need to force yourself into rigid systems that don't work for your brain, plus that you'll probably kick to the curb after a while because you'll feel too trapped. You need support that makes sense for you. Time success doesn't mean perfect punctuality or flawless planning. It means reducing chaos, protecting your energy, and learning to trust your rhythms. And it means stopping the shame spiral. Because all those times you were late or missed a deadline or couldn't follow through, those were not failures. They were signs that you needed a better, more effective system and more compassion.
