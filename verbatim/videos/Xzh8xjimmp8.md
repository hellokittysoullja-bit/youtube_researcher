## 1. Адаптируй экран к стадии пользователя: новый, возвращающийся, вовлечённый
`онбординг · удержание · активация` · [00:22](https://youtu.be/Xzh8xjimmp8?t=22)

**Мысль:** Новому показывают простое приглашение и цель, вернувшемуся готовый план дня, вовлечённому статистику и оптимизацию. Одинаковый экран для всех упускает возможность.

**Слова автора:**

> One, personalize the experience based on user behavior. When you're designing a product, it's important to remember that not all users are the same. Some are brand new, some are coming back after a few uses, and others are deeply engaged. But what many beginner designers do is show everyone the same screen, no matter where they are in their journey. That's a missed opportunity. Designing experiences that adapt to different types of users can make your products feel smarter, more personal, and way more useful. Let me show you what this looks like using a fitness app. The first screen is for a new user. They've just signed up, so the app keeps things really simple. It welcomes them and encourages them to set their weekly goals. Underneath, there are a few trending programs and some easy categories. It's just enough to explore, but not overwhelming, which is exactly what a firsttime user needs. Next, we've got a repeat user. This person has used the app before and is starting to build a routine. Instead of onboarding content, they get a clear workout plan for the day with helpful details like duration and calories burned. Now, here's a super user, someone who's fully engaged and committed to their fitness goals. The app experience becomes more advanced. We start with useful stats like daily steps, calories burned, and heart rate. Then it moves into their workout plan and personalized diet suggestions. At this stage, it's not about helping them get started. It's about helping them optimize and stay on track. Great design meets people where they are.

**Для Напарника:** Дом: новому человеку один вопрос и мягкий старт, вернувшемуся следующий шаг из очереди, а опытному остров и прогресс; всё без стыда.

## 2. Момент намерения: вместо пустого экрана предложи недавнее, популярное, персональное
`чат · запуск-задачи · СДВГ` · [02:26](https://youtu.be/Xzh8xjimmp8?t=146)

**Мысль:** Тап в поиск это момент намерения. Подсказки (недавние, популярные, персональные) не мешают тому, кто знает, что ищет, и снижают трение для неуверенных.

**Слова автора:**

> Think about what happens when a user taps into a search bar. That's a moment of intent. They're looking for something, but they may not know exactly what. That's where your design can do one of two things. Give them a blank screen and expect them to do all the work. Or offer helpful suggestions that make the experience easier, faster, and smarter. Let's look at the first version, the standard blank screen search. Technically, it works, but there's no guidance. It's a dead end unless they know exactly what they want. Now, let's look at a smarter version of the same screen. The search bar is still there, ready to use, but underneath it, the interface offers subtle, useful suggestions. Things like recent searches, so the user can quickly pick up where they left off. Popular items, which gives them a sense of what others are finding useful. Personalized recommendations based on their past behavior or interest. These suggestions don't get in the way. They quietly support the user. And if they already know what they want, they can ignore them and just type. But if they're unsure, these small hints reduce friction and give them somewhere to start.

**Для Напарника:** Пустое поле шага на Фокусе предлагать с подсказками: недавние шаги и следующий шаг из очереди (Zeigarnik), чтобы не начинать с чистого листа.

## 3. Ожидание после решения: уверенное сообщение, структура и визуальный таймлайн
`доверие · иерархия · время` · [03:42](https://youtu.be/Xzh8xjimmp8?t=222)

**Мысль:** Между оплатой и доставкой много неопределённости. Экран с уверенной фразой, человеком-курьером и визуальной временной шкалой снижает стресс и отвечает на вопросы заранее.

**Слова автора:**

> Post-purchase experiences matter just as much as the purchase itself. Think about it. When someone places an order, they've already made a decision. They've paid, committed, and now they're waiting. That moment between payment and delivery is full of expectation and uncertainty. And how you design for that moment says a lot about your product. Now, let's take a look at a very basic order screen. It shows the order number, a plain list of items, some courier info, and a timeline made up of simple dates. Technically, the information is there, but visually there's no structure, no prioritization, no guidance, just data dumped onto a screen. The user has to scan, interpret, and piece everything together themselves. Now, compare that to this upgraded design. From the very first glance, this screen makes you feel like everything is under control. It opens with a confident message. Your order is on the way, paired with clear delivery details and visual icons that help communicate the time window and address instantly. The courier section is no longer just a name and number. It's human. There's a photo, a name, and quick access buttons to call or message, which not only adds trust, but makes the experience feel more personal and responsive. The items, no more long text lists. And one of the best touches, the order history is now a visual timeline. Instead of scanning a block of text and dates, users can instantly see what stage the order is in. This isn't just a UI upgrade. It's a completely different experience because great design doesn't just display information. It reduces stress and answers questions before they're asked.

**Для Напарника:** Экран после старта сессии должен сразу давать уверенность («я рядом, идём») и показывать прогресс визуально, а не только цифрой.

## 4. Экран категорий: единый стиль картинок и цветовые подложки лучше смеси сток-фото
`иерархия · вкус · дизайн-система` · [07:06](https://youtu.be/Xzh8xjimmp8?t=426)

**Мысль:** Различающиеся картинки и плотные тексты поверх них усложняют чтение и выглядят случайно. Мягкие однотонные подложки с единым стилем иллюстраций дают ритм.

**Слова автора:**

> Designers. It looks more designed and might even seem impressive at first glance, but here's the thing. It focuses more on appearance than on usability. Even though there's a dark overlay to help with some text readability, it doesn't fully fix the contrast issue. Some of the text is still hard to read. On top of that, the images don't match. One is bright, another is moody, another feels like a magazine photo. There's no visual consistency. It doesn't feel like these images belong to the same product or brand, more like a random mix of stock photos. And because every block is so visually busy, it takes longer for users to understand what they're looking at. Now, let's look at the third version. And this is where things come together in a much more thoughtful, professional way. This design uses color-coded charts, each with a soft, solid background and a clean, isolated image that represents the category. Because the images are stylistically unified and the background colors are well balanced, the whole screen feels cohesive and branded. There's a rhythm to the layout that makes scanning effortless.

**Для Напарника:** Выбор настроения или типа задачи оформлять одинаковыми по стилю карточками с мягкой подложкой, а не фото.

## 5. Метод ввода зависит от частоты: разовый выбор колесом, частый ввод текстом
`запуск-задачи · мобильный · СДВГ` · [08:33](https://youtu.be/Xzh8xjimmp8?t=513)

**Мысль:** Для разовой настройки уместны колесо и ползунок, для частого и точного ввода нужны поля и степперы. Важно снижать трение, а не выбирать красивое.

**Слова автора:**

> First, consider the type of input. There are two kinds of numeric input scenarios. onetime setup like entering your weight when first signing up or setting your age, height or weight and frequent repeated entry like locking food to track calories or macros. These are completely different use cases and they require completely different UI patterns. Let's look at the screen where the user is asked to enter personal details like weight or height. In these cases, scroll wheels and sliders work beautifully. Why? Because users aren't entering this data often. They just want to get through the setup quickly and these values usually fall within a known range. And most importantly, they are low effort. Users don't need to type or think too much. Now, let's compare that to logging your food every day to track calories or macros. This is where sliders and scroll wheels break down. They're too slow, too fiddly, and don't offer the kind of control users need. If you want to log food quantity like 350 g, I don't want to scroll endlessly or try to drag a slider to the perfect number. Text fields are better here because they're faster, more precise, and less frustrating when used repeatedly. So, how do you choose? Here is a simple rule of thumb. Use sliders or scroll wheels for casual one-time inputs where precision isn't critical. Use text fields, steppers, or number inputs for frequent, precise, or repetitive tasks. It's not about which component looks nicer. It's about reducing friction and matching the input method to the context.

**Для Напарника:** Длительность на Фокусе выбирать чипами (быстро, редко меняется), а текст шага вводить полем; для минут избегать скролл-колёс.
