## 1. Ручная редактура 12000 слов до 2500 сделала игру доступной всем
`крафт · вкус · доступность` · [02:14](https://youtu.be/nsg9FZucQts?t=134)

**Мысль:** Одно редакторское решение — оставить только узнаваемые слова — стало одной из главных причин массовости вместо аудитории словарников.

**Слова автора:**

> The original 2013 prototype had about 12,000 five-letter words, including some incredibly obscure ones that nobody had ever heard of. But Pollock herself made this massive editorial contribution. She went through all 12,000 words and manually filtered them down to roughly 2500 everyday commonly recognized words. This single editorial decision is honestly one of the most underrated reasons the game became accessible to the masses instead of just appealing to hardcore dictionary buffs.

**Для Напарника:** Вручную отбирать тексты, названия ориентиров и реплики кота: узнаваемое и тёплое лучше экзотического.

## 2. Эмодзи-сетка: социальная валюта без усилий и без спойлеров
`геймификация · удержание · примеры · метрики` · [03:44](https://youtu.be/nsg9FZucQts?t=224)

**Мысль:** Игроки сами придумали делиться цветными квадратами; автор встроил это в игру. Делиться легко одним тапом и это выглядит хорошо, а соцдоказательство в ленте сделало остальное.

**Слова автора:**

> Then the real historical turning point hit in November 2021, and it was sparked by a small group of friends over in New Zealand. They'd figured out this clever workaround. They were manually typing out colored square emojis on Twitter to recreate their daily grid. It let them show off their gray, yellow, and green results without actually spoiling the word for anyone else. WLE noticed this organic behavior and brilliantly decided to just build the emoji sharing generator right directly into the game. This simple little feature unlocks some seriously deep psychology. It perfectly tapped into Jonah Burgger's concept of social currency, which basically says, "People share things that make them look good to others, especially when it requires zero extra effort." Wordle's emoji grid gave players this effortless, spoiler-free way to visually flex their intelligence to their entire social network with just a single tap. And when you combine that with Robert Seielini's principle of social proof, the explosion makes complete sense. When your entire Twitter feed fills up with identical colored boxes every single morning, your brain instantly registers it as, "Oh, wow. Everyone is already doing this." Not participating suddenly feels like you're missing out on a massive cultural moment. Those 1.2 million grids shared on Twitter in early January created an irresistible FOMO that brought millions of new players.

**Для Напарника:** Возможность поделиться красивым снимком острова без спойлеров и текста; тихая, добровольная, в один тап.

## 3. Один пазл в день: дефицит, три минуты и сложность на 4-6% выше навыка
`привычки · время · награды · игровой-дизайн` · [05:11](https://youtu.be/nsg9FZucQts?t=311)

**Мысль:** Ограничение до одного пазла в сутки сделало игру ритуалом; обратная связь мгновенная, сложность в потоке (4-6% выше навыка), а счётчики серий делают пропуск дорогим.

**Слова автора:**

> But wait, sharing alone doesn't explain why people kept coming back day after day. Most successful apps want you scrolling endlessly, right? They want infinite content. Well, Wordle intentionally broke that golden rule, relying instead on the scarcity effect. This is the psychological principle where we consistently assign a higher perceived value to things that are limited in availability by restricting players to just one identical puzzle a day. For basically 3 minutes, Wordle transformed a standard word game into a highly anticipated global ritual. Inside that tiny 3minute window, it delivered these perfectly calibrated dopamine hits. Every single guess gives you instant visual feedback. The unpredictable flip of those gray, yellow, and green tiles that directly mirrors BF Skinner's variable reward patterns. It's kind of like a slot machine. Plus, the difficulty sits right in a psychological flow state. It requires just 4 to 6% more skill than your current ability, making it super satisfying instead of frustrating. Add in those double-digit street counters, and the psychological cost of skipping even one day becomes incredibly high.

**Для Напарника:** Короткий ритуал (одна сессия-ритуал в день как мягкий якорь), сложность шага чуть выше текущего. Часть про серии не применима: стрик-давление противоречит тону.

## 4. Неповторимый секрет: делать для одного человека, а не для метрик
`этика · доверие · вкус · мотивация` · [07:22](https://youtu.be/nsg9FZucQts?t=442)

**Мысль:** Типичный дизайн спрашивает, как максимизировать вовлечение; Wordle спрашивал, как сделать что-то милое для одного человека. Аудитория чувствует разницу, а сдержанность нельзя сфабриковать.

**Слова автора:**

> Almost all of them have failed. Why? Because of a fundamental difference in intent. Typical app design asks, "How do we maximize engagement and retention?" But Wordle's design asked, "How do I make something genuinely lovely for one person?" At scale, those two questions might produce similar psychological mechanics, but audiences instinctively sense the difference. You simply cannot manufacture genuine restraint. We all know the difference between a product engineered to hack our attention and one built purely out of care. So, the next time you're building something, ask yourself, does it need to be engineered to perform, or does it just need total genuine honesty? The ultimate takeaway from the whole whle phenomenon is that sometimes the most powerful psychology in the world isn't manipulation or growth hacking or artificial virality. Sometimes the real secret to reaching millions is simply restraint, simplicity, and building something completely true for just one person. If you can do that well enough, the millions will recognize it when they see it.

**Для Напарника:** Принцип Напарника: сделан с заботой для одного человека с СДВГ, без хаков внимания; проверять каждую механику на честность.
