# How to Make OCs People Care About

**Канал:** Ullaiin · 19 мин · субтитры: en (авто YouTube) · https://youtu.be/nITc8FP0zyY

> «Слова автора» — дословный текст из транскрипта (убраны только um/uh и заикания; в авто-субтитрах мало знаков препинания). «Мысль» и «Для Напарника» — вывод разметчика, это не речь автора.

## 1. Личность — то, что аудитория видит 99% времени
`персонаж · маскот · тексты` · [01:27](https://youtu.be/nITc8FP0zyY?t=87)

**Мысль:** Личность определяет, как персонаж взаимодействует с другими; обаятельный на экране персонаж с посредственной историей нравится больше технически лучше написанного.

**Слова автора:**

> I would argue a character's personality is the most important aspect, as this is what defines how a character interacts with others and is what the audience sees 99% of the time. I mean, there are some characters that have mediocre backstories, but because I enjoy seeing them on screen so much, I prefer them a hundred times more to the technically better written characters. With that said, I'll take Pinkie Pie over the best written character any day.

**Для Напарника:** Приоритет для кота: голос, реакции и привычки важнее лора. Вкладываться в тон реплик, а не в сложную предысторию.

## 2. Трoп как короткий путь к узнаванию плюс неожиданный поворот
`персонаж · маскот · вкус` · [02:41](https://youtu.be/nITc8FP0zyY?t=161)

**Мысль:** Зритель видит пост секунды, поэтому знакомый трoп (качок, ботаник) мгновенно рисует образ, а добавленный противоречивый признак (бабушки-охотницы на монстров) делает уникальным. Аудитория достраивает остальное сама.

**Слова автора:**

> Think having a character fall into a common trope is a bad thing. In fact, I often build off of it. If you're an artist posting online in a non-web tune or story format, oftentimes people will only have brief encounters with your character. Usually, people see one post and move on. So, it's hard to fully communicate the intricacies of your character at a glance, much less capture their attention enough to do that. That's why I like using established tropes for my characters. There's a reason they exist, after all. If I say my OC is a jock or nerd, you can instantly picture what that character may be like based on other characters with that trope in other pieces of media. I didn't have to say much of anything, and you can probably already imagine what they act like or even look like. But in order to not make a cliche character, I usually add on to that trope with other traits, sometimes contradicting. For example, in one of my concept art classes, we were tasked with making a group of characters. I decided to make a group of monster hunting granny's contrasting the usually docile and sweet image people have of grandmas with a violent and gritty profession. Each character builds off of different grandmas you may have seen or encountered, but combined with the monster hunting twist. This granny is based on a crazy cat lady stereotype, but each cat she owns is a different type of cat monster. This other grandma is a sweet gardener type, but she uses her shears as a weapon and has a monster fanny pack. And this grandma was based on both wealthy older woman and woman who go crazy for Ray Dun Pottery wearing a werewolf as a fashion statement. With this example, you can see how I use different tropes as a basis for these characters. And with an added twist, they become much more unique than the stereotypes they're based on. Not only that, but because their basis is in tropes, I'm sure you're able to imagine what each character is like without me having to say much. You can probably imagine your grandma or another older woman you encountered who is like one of these ladies and project your experiences onto them. I think that's really the power of using tropes as an artist because it allows your audience to fill in the gaps and take a lot out of a single image if they're just scrolling online and happen to see your work.

**Для Напарника:** Кот-напарник: узнаваемый архетип «тёплый сосед у костра» плюс пара неожиданных черт; пользователь достраивает характер сам.

## 3. Причуды и контрасты делают персонажа живым
`персонаж · маскот · вкус` · [04:39](https://youtu.be/nITc8FP0zyY?t=279)

**Мысль:** Мелкие странности (ест кукурузу по зёрнышку, боится пуговиц) отличают от шаблона; суровый герой, любящий сладкое, сразу подкупает.

**Слова автора:**

> I think adding fun quirks to your character can really make their personality feel much more unique and add to their realism. I mean, I'm sure you have your own little personality quirks, or at least know someone who does. I knew someone who ate corn on the cob by eating each individual kernel at a time, and someone who had a phobia of buttons. These small traits people have are what makes them unique, and it can really help differentiate your OC from others while making them feel like an actual person. It can also make characters feel less tropey or divert from certain tropes. I'm sure many of us know hundreds of stoic brooding characters. I'm usually not a fan of those types of characters, but if you have that stoic, brooding badass who loves cute things or sweets, then I'm immediately charmed. So, give your characters quirks. Give them likes, dislikes, or hobbies that might fit into their personality or possibly contrast it. Give them things they do when they're nervous or weird things they like to collect. It may seem like a small thing, but it adds so much to their character and differentiates them from other OC's.

**Для Напарника:** Мелкие причуды кота (коллекционирует листья, мурлычет у огня, любит определённый час), проявляющиеся в редких репликах и позах.

## 4. Хорошая предыстория объясняет настоящее, а не вызывает жалость
`персонаж · тексты · этика` · [07:47](https://youtu.be/nITc8FP0zyY?t=467)

**Мысль:** Травма как способ вызвать жалость делает персонажа плоским; хорошая предыстория объясняет, почему персонаж такой сейчас, и питает его мотивацию и личность.

**Слова автора:**

> My theory is that a lot of people who write OC's with traumatic backstories often do it for pity, but good writers include trauma as a form of character building and to explain a character's actions. I've certainly encountered characters who fit the trauma-ridden character stereotype, and a lot of their entire personality and character revolve around their backstory, which is just trauma. It just seems more for the purpose of getting others to feel bad for their OC. But it's harder to empathize with a character that doesn't really have much else going for them. Jinx is often haunted by her past both physically and mentally, but we also see other sides of her. She continues her passion for advention from childhood, and we get to see her be fun and playful as well as depressed and angry. Despite seeming quote unquote crazy, we can understand a lot of her actions through her backstory, and they lay the groundwork for who she is in the present. I bring this up because I feel like the difference between a good backstory and a bad backstory is how it affects the character presently. A good backstory should explain why a character is the way that they are and feed into their motivations and personality.

**Для Напарника:** Лор кота и острова должен объяснять нынешнее поведение («почему сидит рядом»), а не драматизировать; тон без жалости и вины.

## 5. Цикл: личность, затем предыстория по теме, затем уточнение личности
`персонаж · процесс · крафт` · [09:23](https://youtu.be/nITc8FP0zyY?t=563)

**Мысль:** Сначала базовая личность, затем предыстория вокруг общей темы, затем уточнение личности через неё; цикл повторяется. Это даёт согласованность и подсказки для идей.

**Слова автора:**

> That's why I think backstory and personality are so intertwined. And if you're writing an OC, you should try to build them both together. A lot of the time, I'll start with a character's base personality, figuring out generally how they act, so I have an idea of who they are in the present before looking to the past. After that point is when I think about backstory, usually considering a general theme I want to go for. For example, the original concept for my character Jun was a Yakuza turned babysitter, and I wanted his personality to kind of be like a stereotypical delinquent. From that concept, I figured I wanted his backstory and general character to revolve around themes of toxic masculinity and discrimination. Since the Yakuza is focused on hierarchy and is male focused, whereas caring for children is often seen as women's work, I could use this as a basis for those themes. I also consider the history of Koreans in the Yakuza and how discrimination against Koreans in Japan pushed many of them into joining the Yakuza due to lack of opportunities. From this, I was able to write his backstory. That being how poor circumstances living in Japan as a Korean pushed him to join the Yakuza and how he developed his toxic traits from both the culture of the Yakuza and the need to appear strong in order to avoid discrimination or being looked down on. You can already see how his backstory feeds into his character, defining the way he acts. And having a base idea of what themes I want to touch on, all aspects of his backstory and personality can feed into that and stay consistent. Using that backstory as a basis, I was then able to refine his personality more. Deciding to make him someone who often lies about his achievements and tries to appear cool at all times, which makes sense due to his backstory of needing to be tough in order to avoid ridicule, which was reinforced both by his upbringing and prior profession. Having that system of making a base personality, deciding a backstory, and then refining the personality using the backstory has been a great system for me to be able to figure out what a character's backstory should be and how to keep their character consistent.

**Для Напарника:** Описать для кота центральную тему (присутствие без надзора) и выводить из неё реплики, привычки и жесты.

## 6. Показывай персонажа за действием, а не статичным портретом
`персонаж · маскот · анимация` · [14:16](https://youtu.be/nITc8FP0zyY?t=856)

**Мысль:** Люди смотрят несколько секунд, поэтому занятие (кофе, лежит в постели с деталями) раскрывает личность больше, чем портрет.

**Слова автора:**

> Firstly, I think the biggest mistake I see a lot of artists make is just posting static photos of their characters. Sure, it might be well executed and look nice, but how much can a portrait tell you about a character? When you post, people are only looking at your work for a few seconds. So, you want to make those seconds count. I found that my posts are more successful when I'm showing my character doing something, anything really. They could be pouring a cup of coffee in the morning, eating a snack, working their job, or interacting with a friend. Drawing your OC doing something can really show more of their personality than a plain portrait can. In fact, one of my most popular posts on Twitter is my OC just lying in bed. But even in such a simple drawing, you can see how some of his personality shows and the way he's lying with the toes hanging out of his mouth. I also added small elements like silly socks and a fun tie hanging in the back. He only uses kids cutlery because he has a fear of sharp objects, so I included that as well. Even the newspaper title Top Five Shades of Gray tells you a bit about his personality and that he has many mundane interests. Drawing your OC in the middle of an action not only adds visual interest, but can tell a lot about your OC's personality through their body language and how they perform an action.

**Для Напарника:** Кот в экране «Дом» и «Фокус» всегда чем-то занят (греется, помешивает костёр, наблюдает), а не стоит неподвижно.

## 7. Динамика между персонажами раскрывает личность лучше слов
`персонаж · маскот · body-doubling` · [15:34](https://youtu.be/nITc8FP0zyY?t=934)

**Мысль:** Взаимодействие двоих проявляет черты каждого; весёлые динамики притягивают.

**Слова автора:**

> Even better than that, drawing your OC interacting with others is even better at showing their personality. And showing a dynamic between two characters can make your audience even more endeared to them. I often draw my two demon characters, Raz and Damon, together because you're able to see more aspects of their personality together. Damon being the unserious and violence loving boss, whereas Raz is more nononsense and hardworking. Their interactions bring out each of those traits in each other, and drawing them interacting helps me explain their personalities and dynamic far better than words ever could. Fun character dynamics are always something that people gravitate towards. So, drawing your OC's together can really help outsiders easily understand and love your characters.

**Для Напарника:** Кот и существо у костра (или пользователь и кот) как пара: реплики и позы раскрывают характер через взаимодействие.

## 8. Крючок в паре слов: «OC-терапевт», «якудза-нянька»
`персонаж · лендинг · конверсия · тексты` · [16:26](https://youtu.be/nITc8FP0zyY?t=986)

**Мысль:** Короткое описание-крючок на основе трoпа мгновенно задаёт ожидание и удерживает зрителя достаточно долго, чтобы узнать глубже; контекст объясняет детали.

**Слова автора:**

> A lot of times when I post character sketch dumps on social media, I try to have a very brief description of my character somewhere in the post. For example, I usually refer to my OC Stanley as my therapist OC and June as my Yakuza babysitter OC. But why is this so important? This goes back to the personality section and character tropes. Like I mentioned before, tropes can help you immediately understand a character and already imagine what they may be like. Usually, these descriptions act as hooks for my characters, intended to capture the attention of my audience in a few words and make them want to know more about my OC's. Of course, they don't encompass the entirety of my OC's character, but sometimes knowing how to simplify your character into a quick hook can really help make your audience stay long enough to know your characters more in depth. You don't even have to describe the characters directly. Sometimes they use hooks for the world or dynamic like Raz and Damon are my hell office auoc's and these two characters are usually described as villain x henchmen. It's kind of like how tags are used in movies and books to peique your interest. That's usually what I use them for and to add more context to the art I make of my characters. After all, if you know Stanley is a therapist going into these sketches, it might make more sense why he has mugs with motivational cats on them and dresses the way that he does.

**Для Напарника:** Лендинг и онбординг: одна ёмкая фраза-крючок про кота-напарника («кот, который сидит рядом, пока ты делаешь дело»).
