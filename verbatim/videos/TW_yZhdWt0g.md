## 1. Витамин или болеутоляющее: продукт, закрывающий психологический зуд
`привычки · удержание · мотивация` · [01:17](https://youtu.be/TW_yZhdWt0g?t=77)

**Мысль:** Продукт становится необходимым, когда снимает неприятное состояние (скука, одиночество, неопределённость).

**Слова автора:**

> Comparison, vitamins versus painkillers. Think about it. When a lot of tech products first launch, they feel a lot like vitamins. They're nice to have, maybe they enrich your life a bit, but you don't truly need them. However, once a product successfully forms a habit, it morphs into a painkiller. And by pain, we're talking about a psychological itch, like the sudden discomfort of boredom, or loneliness, or uncertainty. The product suddenly becomes a must-have remedy that provides instant relief from that itch.

**Для Напарника:** Позиционировать Напарника как болеутоляющее для «не могу начать»: быстрое облегчение в состоянии застревания.

## 2. Перегруженная главная Twitter 2009 против простейшего действия
`запуск-задачи · онбординг · иерархия` · [03:07](https://youtu.be/TW_yZhdWt0g?t=187)

**Мысль:** Мотивация дорога и тяжело меняется, а лёгкость действия решает; перегруженный экран заставляет читать и осмыслять до действия.

**Слова автора:**

> Once a user is prompted, they have to actually do something. And this action has to be the absolute simplest behavior done in anticipation of reward. Twitter's 2009 homepage is actually a brilliant example of what not to do here. If you remember it, it was highly cluttered. There's text everywhere, links, an embedded video, a search bar. It's just overwhelming. For a brand new user, this design demanded way too much cognitive effort. It forced them to stop, read, and comprehend before they could do anything, which severely limited their ability to just take action. Twitter quickly learned they had to simplify this drastically. Because here's the formula. For any action to occur, the user needs sufficient motivation and the ability to do it. But motivation, motivation is expensive and really hard to change. Ability on the other hand, making things ridiculously easy to do, like clicking a single button or endlessly scrolling a feed, that is where habit-forming products excel. Make it simple and the action happens. Basically, reducing the effort required to perform an action is infinitely more effective than trying to hype someone up to do it,.

**Для Напарника:** Дом: одно простое действие сверху; не показывать десяток карточек новому человеку.

## 3. Переменная награда: тайна и её разрешение создают тягу
`дофамин · награды · игровой-дизайн` · [04:13](https://youtu.be/TW_yZhdWt0g?t=253)

**Мысль:** Мозг получает удовольствие от разрешения неопределённости; предсказуемая обратная связь тягу не создаёт.

**Слова автора:**

> Which leads us directly into phase three, variable reward. This is where we create a craving. A highly predictable feedback loop, like say opening your fridge and the light turning on, doesn't create a craving. You expect it. But what if a totally different delicious treat magically appeared every single time you open that door? Now you're intrigued. To understand this, think about a baby meeting a dog for the very first time. The baby's initially filled with uncertainty. What is this hairy thing? Is it going to hurt me? But as that mystery resolves and they realize the dog is friendly, that tension releases into an absolute explosion of giggles. Our brains are literally wired to find the resolution of uncertainty deeply satisfying. But what happens a few years later? The child knows exactly how the dog will react. The mystery's gone and the intense novelty completely wears off. The cycle of conflict, mystery, and resolution is as old as storytelling itself. And it's exactly what keeps us hooked. Think about hit TV shows like Breaking Bad. They leverage this exact same variability. Why do we binge watch? Because the writers introduce a massive conflict, they create this agonizing mystery, and right when they finally resolve it, boom, they introduce a brand new problem. The viewer is left in total suspense, chemically craving the resolution that they can only get by letting the next episode play. It's a variable reward system perfectly tuned to human psychology.

**Для Напарника:** Реveal на финале сессии строить как «тайна → разрешение» (что вырастет на острове), а не как плоский результат.

## 4. Конечная и бесконечная вариативность: FarmVille против Twitter
`игровой-дизайн · удержание · награды` · [05:28](https://youtu.be/TW_yZhdWt0g?t=328)

**Мысль:** Когда механика разгадана, игра с конечной вариативностью умирает; контент от пользователей остаётся непредсказуемым долго.

**Слова автора:**

> But here's the catch. Not all variability is created equal. We have finite variability and infinite variability. Remember the game FarmVille? It exploded in popularity, but it relied on finite variability. Eventually, users completely figured out the mechanics, the game became predictable, and millions of people just abandoned it. On the other hand, platforms like Twitter or Pinterest use infinite variability. Because the content is constantly being generated by millions of other users, the stream is always changing. It never gets completely predictable, and that is what sustains user interest for years on end.

**Для Напарника:** Остров должен иметь неисчерпаемое разнообразие находок (много ориентиров, времена суток, редкие события), иначе привыкание.

## 5. Инвестиция и эскалация обязательств: усилие повышает ценность
`привычки · геймификация · удержание` · [06:13](https://youtu.be/TW_yZhdWt0g?t=373)

**Мысль:** Чем больше усилий вложено, тем выше ценность; малые инвестиции улучшают продукт и заряжают следующий триггер.

**Слова автора:**

> This phase is where the user is asked to put a tiny bit of work, something of value, into the system. This could be time, data, or social capital, and it actually improves the service for their next go-around. This heavily relies on a psychological phenomenon called the escalation of commitment. The crazy thing about us humans is that the more effort we put into something, the more we irrationally value it. Think about the first time you tried spicy food or drank black coffee. Your body probably naturally rejected it, right? But, through repeated exposure and effort, you actually learn to value it. Making small investments in a tech product manipulates our perception in the exact same way. When you take the time to curate your Pinterest board, or follow someone on Twitter, or message a friend on a social app, you are actively investing in that platform. And here is the truly brilliant part. These tiny bits of work inherently load the next trigger. Think about it. Sending a message virtually guarantees you'll get a notification when that person replies, which triggers the entire cycle all over again. Your investments make the product better for you, making it harder and harder to leave.

**Для Напарника:** Имя, настройки кота и история острова — вложения, делающие Напарника «своим».

## 6. Hook — суперсила: выбор архитектуры во благо, привычка или зависимость
`этика · привычки · выбор` · [07:29](https://youtu.be/TW_yZhdWt0g?t=449)

**Мысль:** Те же механики могут формировать полезные рутины по оценке самого человека; ответственность лежит на дизайнерах.

**Слова автора:**

> Reader once told Nir Eyal, "If it can't be used for evil, it's not a superpower." Building these hooks is undeniably a superpower. Having the ability to engineer human behavior on a massive scale is incredibly potent. And if product designers use it irresponsibly, they hold the terrifying potential to turn everyday users into mindless zombies, just clicking and scrolling their lives away to alleviate manufactured pain. But, there is a silver lining here, and it's called choice architecture. We can, and honestly must, harness this exact same framework to enhance people's lives. We can design hooks that nudge people to make better choices as judged by themselves. Just imagine applying the Hook Model to create positive routines, apps that improve our relationships, make us smarter, or dramatically increase our productivity and health. Ultimately, technology is only going to become more pervasive and more persuasive. We are no longer just consuming products, they are actively shaping who we are. So, as we look at the apps on our phones and the automatic routines in our own lives, we really have to ask ourselves this profound question. Are you building a healthy habit or an unhealthy addiction?

**Для Напарника:** Критерий для каждой механики Напарника: человек сам скажет, что это ему полезно.
