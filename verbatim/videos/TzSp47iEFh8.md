## 1. Петля создаёт предвкушение: работа сначала тяжёлая, потом работает на тебя
`игровой-дизайн · дофамин · награды · мотивация` · [02:09](https://youtu.be/TzSp47iEFh8?t=129)

**Мысль:** В Splendor сначала много усилий, потом карты работают за тебя, и игрок чувствует прогресс. Повторяющаяся петля даёт планировать и ждать, а план и его исполнение делают награды лучше.

**Слова автора:**

> At first, you'll do a lot of taking gems actions, but as you get more cards, you don't need as many gems, so the ratio is going to change. And so, you're going to feel that feeling of progress. And you're going to have players have in their heads this question of, oh, am I at some point going to get cards for free? And yes, you will. And that's part of like the game structure and what makes the game fun is at first you've got to work really hard to get what you want and then you don't need to work as hard because your cards are working for you. One thing that the core game loop or a loop in general does for games is it can build anticipation. And you can see that actually really well in games with rounds. So, when you have a round, typically, how often are you going to tell players, "Hey, let's do one full round and then you're going to get the game." Well, that happens because you do the same things over and over again. And once you do them once, then players start planning. They start anticipating what's going to happen next round. And the game suddenly gets interesting. You're not going to be doing different actions every single time. You're going to be doing the same actions, but it's going to you're going to be changing the context of those actions. you're going to be changing the power of those actions and those actions are going to be affecting each other which is a big part of what makes games fun. And so game loops can build anticipation because players can see, oh, I'm going to buy a card now and then later that card is going to come which might help me in this other part of the loop which is maybe combat like in Dune Imperium for instance. And then if I win at combat, then that's gonna make buying cards easier because I'm gonna get spice and then I'm going to get that other thing. And so players can start anticipating. They can start planning. One of the types of fun is making a plan and then executing it or making a plan and seeing if it's going to execute. Is great and it makes rewards all the.

**Для Напарника:** Ранние сессии заметно дороже усилием, а накопленное на острове со временем начинает приносить само: чувство прогресса и ожидания.

## 2. Пейсинг: напряжение и разрядка, постепенное усложнение петли даёт ещё один ход
`игровой-дизайн · время · дофамин · удержание` · [05:13](https://youtu.be/TzSp47iEFh8?t=313)

**Мысль:** Хороший темп чередует напряжение и отдых. В Civilization петля растёт шаг за шагом, и уже к концу хочется повторить начало.

**Слова автора:**

> You don't want cinematic scene after cinematic scene after cinematic scene or combat after combat after combat. For good pacing, you want a na natural like tension and then a release of tension and then tension then a release of tension. Or you can see that as action and then rest. Action and then rest. when speaking of pacing, the most famous example of pacing ever is of good pacing is the game civilization. It's famous for that one more turn feeling. So the game loop starts with just you have a unit and on your first turn you're going to choose where to build. So you've got one decision. Second turn your building is built. Your unit is free. Okay. Well now I have a unit and a building. So these are two actions that I can take. So my unit where do I move my unit? My building. What do I make in my building? All right. New turn. my building has built its thing. My unit has moved. and so on. And bit by bit, your game loop gets bigger with more actions and you feel a huge sense of progress. And the coolest thing about it is by the time you get to the end of your game loop, you already feel like playing again those things that were in the beginning of your game loop.

**Для Напарника:** Чередование Фокус -> отдых на острове; каждая следующая сессия добавляет чуть больше действий, а не сразу всё.

## 3. Простая ядровая петля; убирай всё, что её не поддерживает
`процесс · игровой-дизайн · крафт` · [06:45](https://youtu.be/TzSp47iEFh8?t=405)

**Мысль:** Определи самое частое действие игрока и поддерживай его; дизайнер за две сессии вырезал лишнее, и игра стала весёлой и понятной.

**Слова автора:**

> It can be really simple. Like I've heard a lot of people talk about in the video game space about how like a lot of side sidescrollers they see jumping as their main game loop. Like they see just that how do we make moving around in the game fun cuz that's the thing that players are going to be doing most of they're going to be running around the world. And so I think in board games similarly identify those things that players are going to be doing a lot of and make sure that those things are being supported by the game. Like what happened with a designer who came to me with a game that he wasn't sure if it was fun. We identified the core game loop. We did two sessions where we just removed stuff that wasn't supporting the core game loop. And in two sessions, he went from not being sure if it's fun to absolutely the game being fun, testing really well, and it literally getting to a place where it's currently being pitched and the designer is like really proud of it. That can happen when you identify the game's loop. Take out stuff that doesn't support it. And in so many cases, that just immediately frees up the game. It makes it makes it really clear to players what they're doing.

**Для Напарника:** Ядро Напарника: шаг -> Фокус -> рост острова. Любую новую фичу сверять: поддерживает ли она это ядро; иначе убирать.
