## 4. Эффект IKEA и накопленная ценность
`привычки · геймификация · удержание` · [14:12](https://youtu.be/lkjkC8osdgU?t=852)

**Мысль:** Мы переоцениваем то, во что вложили усилия, а накопленные данные делают продукт лучше и дороже в уходе.

**Слова автора:**

> It's a great question. It relies on a psychological principle called the IKEA effect. >> The IKEA effect? >> Yeah. Studies show we irrationally overvalue things that we have put effort into creating ourselves. If you build that wobbly bookshelf from IKEA, you love it more than a pre-assembled one because you invested your labor in it. >> I'm so guilty of this. Look at this masterpiece I built. It wiggles, but it's mine. >> Exactly. When you invest in a product, you value it more. But there's a second, more practical reason for investment. Store value. >> Okay. What's that? >> The more you use a product, the better it gets for you personally. >> Oh, like my Spotify algorithm. >> Perfect example. Every time you heart a song or make a playlist, you are investing. You're teaching the algorithm and all that data becomes stored value. If you left Spotify now, you'd lose all of that. You'd have to start from scratch. >> So, it creates a lock in. >> It was harder to leave because I've left so much of myself in there. >> It creates high switching costs. Think about Twitter or Instagram. Your investment is your follower count. You can't just take that with you to a new platform. That investment anchors you to the service.

**Для Напарника:** Остров строится усилиями человека (его сессии); это его вещь, поэтому он важен.

## 5. Фасилитатор: психология помогает делать то, что человек и так хочет
`этика · мотивация · стрики` · [16:02](https://youtu.be/lkjkC8osdgU?t=962)

**Мысль:** Те же триггеры, стрики и социальные награды этичны, если помогают человеку в его собственной цели (пример: Bible app, Duolingo).

**Слова автора:**

> He introduces something called the manipulation matrix to help creators figure this out. >> A matrix. Okay. How does it work? How do we know if we're being evil? It asks two simple questions. One, does this product materially improve the user's life? And two, does the maker use the product themselves? >> Okay, so let's walk through the four quadrants. >> All right. If you don't use it yourself and it doesn't improve the user's life, you are a dealer. >> O, a dealer. That's a loaded term. >> It is. It implies exploitation. Like a drug dealer. You know it's bad. You don't touch it, but you sell it to others to get rich. That's where you find casinos or maybe certain predatory games. >> What if you don't use it, but you think it helps people? >> Then you're a peddler. This is often where you find, say, altruistic advertising or some educational apps made by people who don't really engage with the material, it usually lacks authenticity and it often fails. >> Okay? And if you use it, but it doesn't actually improve lives, >> then you're an entertainer. Like a casual game or a TV show, there's nothing inherently wrong with that, but it's just entertainment. It's fleeting. >> So the golden quadrant, yeah, the goal is to be a facilitator. >> The facilitator, you use the product yourself and you genuinely believe it materially improves the user's life. This is where I wants creators to be. >> So an app like Duolingo or a fitness tracker. >> Exactly. Or the Bible app, which is a case study in the book. They use the exact same psychology, triggers, streaks, social rewards, but they're helping you do something you already want to do. That feels like a really key distinction. Using psychology to help someone do what they want to do versus trapping them into doing something they don't.

**Для Напарника:** Оправдывает механики роста при условии, что они помогают человеку исполнять его собственную цель, без штрафов.
