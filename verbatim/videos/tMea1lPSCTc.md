## 1. Силы прогресса (Jobs to be Done): боль и притяжение против привычки и тревоги
`онбординг · мотивация · процесс` · [00:24](https://youtu.be/tMea1lPSCTc?t=24)

**Мысль:** Две силы толкают к изменению (боль текущего и притяжение нового), две блокируют (привычка и тревога перед новым); онбординг должен усиливать первые и снижать вторые.

**Слова автора:**

> To build a high converting onboarding experience, one useful concept is the progress making forces from the Jobs that we've done framework. According to this framework, there are four main forces that influence users as they go from the Current Situation to a New Behavior. There are two forces that direct users to adopt a new behavior. That's a portion of the current situation whether it's painful for them or the pull of the new solution, whether it's innovative or something new. And there are also two forces that block change. This is the habit of the present and the anxiety of the new solution,.

**Для Напарника:** У человека с СДВГ главная блокирующая сила это тревога начала; интерфейс и тон кота должны снимать её, а не добавлять давления.

## 2. Мгновенно покажи результат, который хочет человек (Wave подбирает цвета бренда)
`онбординг · доверие · анимация` · [02:26](https://youtu.be/tMea1lPSCTc?t=146)

**Мысль:** Автоматическое оформление счёта под логотип визуализирует итог, даёт «вау» и уверенность в продукте; это проверено в интервью с клиентами.

**Слова автора:**

> Is as you upload your logo to set up your invoice, they automatically identify your brand colors and update the invoice template to match your brand. It's like magic. It's a brilliant way to show users what they really want here, which is a professional and beautiful invoice without all the pain and hustle. I got a chance to talk to Vivek, the Director of Growth at Wave, and he provides some insights as to the effectiveness of this visualization. - So this is a piece that we validated, but actually talking to a whole bunch of customers during our deep dive and customer discovery. Providing this visual assurance when they actually see it happen. "Wow, this is great. It's beautiful. Also, it's very professional." That gives them a lot of confidence that this is a good product, first of all, something that I can trust.

**Для Напарника:** Сразу после ввода шага или имени показать видимую реакцию мира (кот кивает, у костра что-то меняется), чтобы человек увидел результат.

## 3. Предвосхищай возражения и не удивляй: предупреди и объясни причину просьбы
`онбординг · доверие · этика · тексты` · [03:11](https://youtu.be/tMea1lPSCTc?t=191)

**Мысль:** Сюрпризы вроде карты на третьем шаге вызывают тревогу; Userlist предупреждает заранее дважды и называет причину в пользу человека; возражения брать из тикетов поддержки.

**Слова автора:**

> Second, you need to break the objection. Knowing that new users are still skeptical. Another mark of high converting user onboarding experience is anticipating and addressing objections and users before they come up. A perfect example of this is Userlist, a customer messaging platform for SaaS. On the pricing page, one question a prospective user might ask is, "Do I get all or some of the features during the free trial?" They address this right away by stating in the subheadline copy that all features are available starting from day one. Another big objection of signing-up for a free trial is requiring a credit card upfront. Userlist does require a credit card to sign-up for a free trial, but they give new users advanced notice that there'll be requiring a credit card right upfront twice before people even sign-up. Once on the FAQ section of the pricing page, they state that their product is free to try, but they ask for credit card details upfront to prevent spam and providing a better onboarding experience. They also give new users heads up on the first step of the sign-up process, reiterating this message. I really love what Userlists did here. They did two things exceptionally well to break objections. First, they let ahead that you need to put your credit card to start a trial. So you're not surprised when you get to the third step in the sign-up process when you enter your credit card details. Things like this make users feel anxiety, and you don't want that what we saw with the progress making forces. That's one of the things that pull users away from adopting your product. So make sure you don't surprise new users and stay upfront, whatever you're doing, whether that's asking for a credit card upfront. Second, they provide a reason why they ask for a credit card for a free trial. When you're asking users to do something that's hard, providing a reason really helps that in this case, they say, "That is to prevent spam and provide a better onboarding experience." I really love the second part of this because it's something that new users want. They wanna better onboarding experience. And by stating it in terms of the value for the user is as much about what Userlist is getting as much as what users are getting. It makes it easier to swallow this bitter pill.

**Для Напарника:** Любую просьбу (имя, уведомления, разрешения) заранее объявлять и объяснять смысл голосом кота; никаких сюрпризов.

## 4. Закрепление привычки: найди «магическое число» (Facebook 7 друзей, Slack 2000 сообщений)
`удержание · метрики · привычки` · [05:52](https://youtu.be/tMea1lPSCTc?t=352)

**Мысль:** Недостаточно первого aha: у успешных продуктов есть порог вовлечённости, после которого удержание резко растёт.

**Слова автора:**

> Third, high-converting user onboarding experiences commit the change. As soon as new users get to the first aha moment or pay the first invoice, your job is not done. With user onboarding, your primary goal is to get users to break the old habits and fall in love with your product so much that they can't imagine their life without it. You have to help users commit new habits. In Malcolm Gladwell's book, "The Tipping Point," Gladwell defines this as a tipping point, it's when an idea or product stays in the mind of the consumer and influences their future behavior. In Silicon Valley startups, this moment is often called The Magic Number. For Facebook, adding one friend is not enough. The Growth Team found that getting new users to add seven friends in the first 10 days increases the likelihood of that user becoming a longterm active user. Now, Slack Founder, similar magic number in an interview with First Round Review. Here's what Steward Butterfield, Slack's Co-founder describes it as, "Based on our experiences of which companies stuck with us and which didn't, we decided that any team that has exchanged 2,000 messages, 93% of those customers are still using Slack today." It's not enough to just send one message to a colleague with Slack, Butterfield, and a Slack team found that the tipping point is when a Slack Group collective has sent 2000 messages. Now Twitter has also something similar, Josh Elman, who worked growth at Twitter and is now a partner at Greylock Partners, has said, "If Twitter couldn't get a person to follow 30 other people, that person was very unlikely to ever come back.".

**Для Напарника:** Определить для Напарника свой порог (например, N завершённых сессий за первые дни) и вести человека к нему мягко, без стрик-давления.
