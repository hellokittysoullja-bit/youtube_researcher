## 1. Система 1 и 2: 90% решений принимает подсознание
`нейро · исследования · мотивация` · [01:43](https://youtu.be/m8XBkwLPgLQ?t=103)

**Мысль:** Эмоции решают, а сознание только оправдывает; быстрые решения — подсознание, медленные требуют усилий.

**Слова автора:**

> In Nobel Prize winner Daniel Kahneman's excellent, excellent book, Thinking, Fast and Slow, he outlines two distinct modes of thinking, system one and system two. System one, he postulates, is thoughts from the subconscious mind. They're basically fast thoughts. Think of things like understanding and hearing language. System two conscious thinking, on the other hand, is from the conscious mind. It's slow, it's robust, and it's far more effortful. Think of things like learning a new language, or creating something new, or parking in a tight spot. Higher-order thinking is essentially where system two thinking comes in. Now, we all know this fact at some level, but we don't like to think that we're all very rational creatures who make our decisions after careful, conscious deliberation. But that is far, far from the truth. As it turns out, about 90% of decisions we make throughout the day are from the system one subconscious responses. Our emotions are making the decisions for us, and all our conscious mind can do is basically justify those reasons.

**Для Напарника:** Первые секунды экрана решают: эмоциональный тон и красота важнее аргументов и объяснений.

## 2. Опросы дают поверхностные ответы: люди не знают своих мотивов
`исследования · метрики · вкус` · [02:51](https://youtu.be/m8XBkwLPgLQ?t=171)

**Мысль:** Не ложь, а автопилот: люди не осознают подсознательные процессы, поэтому нужно смотреть на поведение.

**Слова автора:**

> Traditional market research essentially depends on focus groups and surveys and interviews, all of which depend on explicitly stated responses from people, which is extremely, extremely limited. When you get a group of people within a room and ask them to look at a certain product or watch a certain ad, for example, you get very good surface-level conscious responses. But when you try and understand the subconscious responses, this technique fails miserably. That is why when you try and pry information from someone, you get overly generic or overly positive responses from people. Now, it's not because people are lying to you or they're trying to deceive you, but as I mentioned, we as humans are largely running on autopilot, and we're completely unaware of our subconscious processes that require decision-making. We're completely unaware of our subconscious processes that are driving our desires in the first place.

**Для Напарника:** Оценивать Напарника по поведению (старт сессий, возвраты), а не только по анкетам.

## 3. Отвратительные/пугающие сообщения не работают, позитивные работают
`стыд · нейро · тексты` · [10:37](https://youtu.be/m8XBkwLPgLQ?t=637)

**Мысль:** Шокирующие образы включают инсулу и отворот взгляда; связь с желаемым поведением не возникает, а позитивные выгоды запоминаются лучше.

**Слова автора:**

> So, what we found is that the disgusting images, they activate a part of the brain called the insula. Now, the insula is a deep brain structure that is responsible for visceral responses, responses that you can't really control. So, when you see gross and horrible things, your first immediate primary motivating response is to look away immediately. You don't want to interact with it. It's horrible. It's disgusting. You want to look away as soon as possible. What this does is it fails to connect the rational part of your brain with the message of not smoking. So, no matter how many times these disgusting images are flashed on the screen or they're on cigarette packets in front of you, your brain is not making the connection that this horrible image is going to lead to bad health, or this horrible image leads to not smoking. Your brain just simply does not make that connection in the first place. Shunning that horrible image does not lead to actually shunning of the pack of cigarettes in the first place. Now, what does work then? We all like to discourage people from smoking. What does actually work from neuroscience is positive and direct motivation. What we found time and time again in many different countries across the world is when you communicate a positive message to people, when you communicate that quitting smoking will improve your skin, or it will make you live longer, or you won't be as breathless as often. All of these messages tend to go farther and actually connecting that positive message to people, and they tend to quit smoking a lot quicker and a lot sooner, actually. So, these positive messages are much easier to correlate within the brain and have that connection within the brain in the first place.

**Для Напарника:** Подтверждает тон без стыда и вины: формулировать выгоду («тебе будет легче»), а не угрозу пропуска.

## 4. 100 мс понять, что это реклама; ~3.5 секунды на объявление
`лендинг · внимание · конверсия` · [15:22](https://youtu.be/m8XBkwLPgLQ?t=922)

**Мысль:** За долю секунды человек решает, смотреть ли дальше, поэтому зацепить нужно в первую секунду.

**Слова автора:**

> Basically shows that when you're scrolling through Facebook and Instagram, and you come across an ad, essentially it takes you 100 milliseconds to figure out that you're watching an ad in the first place. Just 100 milliseconds. It takes you less than 400 milliseconds for your brain to already start forming responses to that. And it takes you less than a second to basically figure out whether you're going to continue watching that ad in the first place or not. Overall, we find that people tend to spend less than 3.5 seconds on any digital ad in the first place. That is an extremely short amount of time. So, what this does is this challenges marketers to a massive extent. What it does is basically it makes marketers hook the consumers within the first second itself, or lose to the scrolling completely.

**Для Напарника:** Первый экран лендинга должен показывать суть и кота сразу, без раскачки.

## 5. Задержка загрузки на 2–3 секунды даёт стресс как от фильма ужасов
`мобильный · метрики · крафт` · [16:18](https://youtu.be/m8XBkwLPgLQ?t=978)

**Мысль:** Буферизация повышает пульс на 44%; при таком стрессе эмоциональной вовлечённости не будет.

**Слова автора:**

> And there's a horrible 4G reception in the area, and your ad starts buffering, and it doesn't load in the first place. What does that do? Well, we did a study for Ericsson to understand how does that actually stress work within the brain, and we realized that people tend to get very, very, very stressed. In fact, we found that even a slight delay in loading and buffering leads to about a 44% increase in heart rate, which is a massive, massive amount. And we found that even a 2 to 3-second delay that they're facing, a 2 to 3-second delay gives them the same amount of stress that they feel while they were watching a horror movie.

**Для Напарника:** Реплики кота и экраны должны грузиться быстро или показывать мягкий скелетон; задержки ответа ИИ маскировать в характере.
