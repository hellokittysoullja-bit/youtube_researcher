## 1. Закон Якоба: оригинальность в идеях, скука в конвенциях
`дизайн-система · вкус · доверие` · [01:09](https://youtu.be/J7LWfqWbx1o?t=69)

**Мысль:** Люди проводят время на чужих сайтах, поэтому ломать ожидаемые паттерны заставляет думать и рождает сомнение.

**Слова автора:**

> It's called Jacob's Law and it comes from a simple truth about your visitors. They do not spend their time on your website. They spend it on everyone else's. Thousands of hours on those huge shopping sites and apps that they open every single day. So by the time that they reach you their brain already holds a firm expectation of where things should be and how they should behave. The shopping basket belongs on the top right. The logo should always take you home. Now none of that is written down anywhere, but your visitor's brain treats it as law because every other site they've ever used has trained it to do so. A lot of designers try to be original with the parts that realistically need to be invisible. They might try, you know, a seemingly clever navigation that hides where things are or a checkout flow that reinvents the process that people already know. And every time that you break a pattern that the brain expects, you force the visitor to stop and think. And that small jolt of effort renders doubt in the user's mind. Locally, the move here is pretty simple. Be original with your ideas and your craft will be deeply boring with your conventions. So, put the familiar things exactly where the brain already expects to find them so the visitor never has to ask a question that they shouldn't have to ask.

**Для Напарника:** Навигация (нижний таб-бар) и корзина действий должны быть привычными; оригинальность вкладывать в кота и остров, а не в расположение кнопок.

## 2. Закон Фитса на телефоне: главное действие большое и в зоне большого пальца
`мобильный · иерархия · доступность` · [02:51](https://youtu.be/J7LWfqWbx1o?t=171)

**Мысль:** Время достижения цели зависит от размера и расстояния; на мобильном под одним пальцем цена умножается; главное действие должно быть самым лёгким физически.

**Слова автора:**

> So, Fitts's law says that the time it takes someone to reach a target depends on two things: how big the target is and then how far away it is. So, the bigger and closer something is, the faster and more confidently a person will move towards it. And inversely, the smaller and the farther away it is, the more effort it takes. And effort is where people inevitably just give up. On a website, this is the difference between a call to action somebody actually clicks on without having to think and one that they have to aim at. A small button floating in an awkward corner surrounded by other small links is a target that the brain has to work to hit. On a phone, where the whole thing is happening under one moving thumb, the cost of this multiplies massively. Watch how the best mobile experiences handle this. The main action is large. It sits low on the screen inside the natural arc of your thumb. It's not crowded by, you know, 10 competing links at the same size. The single most important thing you want someone to do is also the easiest thing on the page to physically do. So, go and look at your primary call to action button right now on a phone held in one hand. If your thumb has to stretch to the top of the screen or hunt between cramped links, then you're adding friction at the exact moment you're asking someone to take action. So, >> >> make the important target big and bring it into reach.

**Для Напарника:** Кнопку «Начать фокус» держать крупной и низко на экране, без 10 конкурирующих ссылок рядом.

## 3. Социальное доказательство ставить в момент сомнения, а не на отдельной странице
`доверие · конверсия · лендинг` · [04:21](https://youtu.be/J7LWfqWbx1o?t=261)

**Мысль:** Отзывы бесполезны далеко от решения; они работают рядом с действием и когда зеркалят посетителя (его отрасль, проблема).

**Слова автора:**

> Beings are wired to look at what other people are doing before they decide, and that instinct gets stronger the less certain they feel. In an unfamiliar situation, we copy the crowd because the crowd feels safer than just guessing alone. That is social proof, and almost every website uses it badly. The bad version is, let's say, a page called testimonials that nobody visits or just a random wall of five-star quotes sitting far away from any actual decision. The proof, okay, yes, it's technically present, but it's nowhere near that moment of doubt, and so it just doesn't work as well as it can be. One caveat I will add is that you can absolutely have a testimonials page. We literally have it on our website, but if that's the only place that you're showcasing social proof, then you are losing an unimaginable amount of conversions. The brain needs the reassurance at the exact moment of hesitation. So, undeniable social proof belongs next to an action and not buried in an about Review scores belong on the product detail page in the moment somebody is deciding to add something to cart. The same way, you know, the big booking sites show you that a room was booked 11 times today whilst you're looking at it. There's a slightly more subtle layer, too. So, proof is most persuasive when it mirrors the visitor. A quote, let's say, from someone in their industry with their job title facing their problem. It lands far harder than a famous logo that has nothing to do with them. People are reassured most by people like themselves. So, don't just collect proof. You actually need to place it. Walk through your site and find every moment where a visitor pauses and wonders whether this site's safe, and then put the evidence right there in their eyeline at the second that they need it.

**Для Напарника:** На лендинге вплотную к CTA ставить отзывы людей с СДВГ в похожей ситуации, а не общую стену цитат.

## 4. Авторитет работает рядом с призывом и только если реальный
`доверие · лендинг · этика` · [06:35](https://youtu.be/J7LWfqWbx1o?t=395)

**Мысль:** Сертификаты и логотипы должны стоять близко к месту просьбы; фальшивый или пустой бейдж снижает доверие.

**Слова автора:**

> Authority bias is the brain's habit of giving more weight to anyone it reads as credible or qualified. We're trained from childhood to defer to the expert, and that instinct, it doesn't switch off when we open a browser. So, when a visitor sees credible signals, they see certifications, press, recognizable clients, you know, real credentials, the brain upgrades how it trusts everything else on the page. Plenty of sites either they bury their authority or they just waste it. The founder, let's say with 20 years of experience, they bury it three pages deep into the site. The business that's been mentioned somewhere really serious mentions it nowhere near the decision. The credibility exists, but it's not standing where it should be doing the work. Ideally, you should bring your authority signals next to or in close proximity to your asks. Those press logos or the certifications belong close to the point where you want action to be taken, and not aimlessly floating along where it connects to nothing. If a real person with real authority stands behind an offer, the visitor should feel that presence at the moment they decide on whether to trust you. So, ideally, you want credentials in here, too. I personally use the amount of sites that we've built, the awards that we've won, and our client satisfaction rate. Now, this doesn't stand front and center on our own site, but if you're running a founder-led offer, you could look to add this right up in hero section alongside your call to action. One warning because the brain is also good at spotting fakes, borrowed or hollow authority is going to backfire really badly. And what I mean is a meaningless badge or a logo that you can't back or it reads as performative or disingenuous, which is going to dramatically lower trust instead of raising it. Authority only works when it's real and verifiable.

**Для Напарника:** На лендинге: реальные источники (исследования, специалисты) рядом с CTA; никаких выдуманных регалий.

## 5. Эффект умолчания: предвыбранное сохраняют большинство, и это ответственность
`мотивация · этика · цены · тёмные-паттерны` · [08:27](https://youtu.be/J7LWfqWbx1o?t=507)

**Мысль:** Донорство (opt-out ~90%) показывает силу дефолта; ставить по умолчанию то, что честно посоветовал бы другу, иначе выиграешь клик и потеряешь доверие.

**Слова автора:**

> Whatever option you preselect for the visitor, most of them will keep because at the end of the day, we're all wired to take the path of least resistance. The most cited example of this is actually not from the web at all. Countries where organ donation is set up so that you're enrolled unless you opt out have consent rates up around 90% while countries where you have to actively opt in, they sit way, way lower. Same people, same values, only thing that changed was which box was already ticked. Now, let's apply that to a website. A great example is the billing toggle that defaults to annual rather than monthly or the plan that arrives already marked as recommended and preselected for the visitor. That streaming service that plays the next episode unless you stop it. None of these force anyone, they simply set the starting point, and the starting point carries enormous weight because most people just won't opt out of the default. This is also where you carry a responsibility because the same power can be used against people and the brain comes to resent it when it notices. So, as an example, a pre-ticked box that signs someone up to something that they didn't want is the kind of default that, okay, yes, it wins the click, but it loses the trust both in the short and in the long term. The scale and I guess the nuance is to set defaults that serve the visitor. E.g., the plan that fits most people, which is usually the choice that you'd honestly recommend to a friend. What tends to happen here is the easy path and the right path tend to be the same path. So, look across your site, every place where a choice is being made, and then ask yourself what's currently selected by default because in practice, you are making that decision for most of your visitors, whether you intend to or not.

**Для Напарника:** Подтверждает «25 минут — рекомендуем» на Фокусе; никаких предотмеченных подписок.

## 6. Эффект приманки в тарифах: сосед, который делает нужный вариант щедрым
`цены · конверсия · этика` · [10:19](https://youtu.be/J7LWfqWbx1o?t=619)

**Мысль:** Люди судят по сравнению; третий нарочно худший вариант делает целевой явно выгоднее; но очевидный трюк раздражает.

**Слова автора:**

> The decoy effect works like this. So, when people compare options, they're surprisingly bad at judging value on its own. So, what they'll do is they judge by comparison instead. And if you add a third option that is deliberately a little worse than the one you actually want them to choose, you make that target option look obviously better, and therefore, more people are going to pick it. The famous version comes from a magazine subscription that researchers studied. So, what happened was there was a web only option at a low price. There was a print only option, and then there was a print and web option the same price point as the print only one. Almost nobody wanted print on its own. So, why was it there? Because next to it, print and web for the same money looks like an obvious win, and the share of people choosing the expensive bundle jumped dramatically. If you remove the useless middle option, then the choices actually flip. On a pricing page, this is the difference between three tiers of thrown down at random, and then three tiers arranged on purpose. The option that you want most people to take should be sitting next to a neighbor that makes it look really generous. The decoy earns its place purely by flattering the option beside it, and it barely has to sell anything by itself. Now, I do want to be careful here because this is the principle that comes closest to manipulation, and your visitors, they are smart. So, a decoy that is obviously a trick, it's going to annoy people. A pricing structure that genuinely makes your best value option look like what it is, the best value. It's just clarity.

**Для Напарника:** Если будут платные тарифы — расположить так, чтобы честно лучший план выглядел лучшим; не манипулировать.

## 7. Эффект неловкости: признанный недостаток повышает доверие
`доверие · лендинг · этика` · [12:10](https://youtu.be/J7LWfqWbx1o?t=730)

**Мысль:** Безупречный бренд выглядит нереальным; честная мелкая неидеальность делает всё остальное правдоподобным, включая тройки в отзывах и «кому это не подходит».

**Слова автора:**

> The Pratfall effect is it's this finding that admitting a flaw can make people trust you more as opposed to less. >> >> So, a person or a brand that is presented as flawless, it kind of reads as slightly unreal. And so, the brain stays on guard. The moment a small, honest imperfection appears, the whole thing becomes a little bit more believable. And believable is what people actually trust. So, almost every website does the opposite. Every review is five stars. Every outcome is perfect. Product is for everyone, and there's nothing it can't do. And a visitor who's been on the internet for more than a week obviously reads that wall of perfection as exactly what it is. It's marketing 101, and so they brace themselves. Now, this is tough if you literally only have five-star reviews. But, if you have some less than stellar ones, don't be afraid to use them. So, let's run a demo. Picture the opposite, right? A site that shows its real review average, including those pesky threes, where you can read the honest criticism and see exactly how the company handled it. A brand that's confident enough to tell you right there on the pricing page who this is not for, right? That honesty, it does something that no amount of polish can buy. It makes every other claim on the site so much more believable because clearly this is a business that tells you the truth even when it stings them a little bit.

**Для Напарника:** На лендинге честно писать, кому Напарник не подойдёт и чего не умеет; без пустого «поможет всем».
