## 1. Четыре категории риска (Каган): usability, feasibility, business, value

**Мысль:** Дизайнер обычно зациклен на usability-риске, а команда думает о четырёх рисках сразу, и они пересекаются; общий язык рисков выравнивает команду.

**Слова автора:**

> Number one is rally around the same risks so when you're designing a building product every decision that you make is going to carry some amount of risk and in marty kagan's book inspired he explicitly calls out four major risk categories and i've been using his framework for years now and anecdotally it has been so helpful for my day-to-day work not only actual designing piece of it but in influencing and communicating and collaborating all right so take a look here are the four risk categories i'm not going to go into real detail here i highly recommend that you check out the book inspired by marty kagan he does a great job of explaining these and going into detail and breaking everything down but for the purpose of this video and just also the way i communicate this in meetings and in presentations i'm going to do it that way just to make it easy so let's go bottom up usability risk this is about if the user can actually figure out what you designed or built pretty straightforward right this is kind of in the wheelhouse of designers now feasibility risk is more about capabilities can the builders engineers actually make it code it build it business risk can the business work with it i know that's a little bit vague but typically it deals with like legal financial you know maybe there's some regulatory or compliance thing going on and the most important probably is going to be value risk so even if everything else checks out and you design it and build it and put it out into the world will the users actually like really buy it or will they actually use it if you make it for them and it's helpful to know that these are all related it's they're not mutually exclusive so there's going to be overlaps also if you change one or you adjust anything it may impact some other risk category.

## 2. Говори на языке рисков других ролей

**Мысль:** Практика: учить язык бизнес-, ценностного и технического риска, понять какой риск важен какой роли, и свести команду к общему описанию смеси рисков.

**Слова автора:**

> So the problem i want to point out here is that early on i had this issue and i see junior designers having this issue and it's that we focus too much on the usability risk now i'm not saying don't focus on it because it's important it's part of the equation but you can imagine other roles prioritizing and really thinking more about the other types of risks now this creates an issue when you're trying to get on the same page about what the problem is and how you're going to solve it so here's what i would suggest and this is something that i've practiced over the years and i am still working on so number one speak to the other types of risks practice it try to see if you can understand what a business risk is for your company what is a value risk for your product or what is a feasibility risk for the engineers that you work with and see if you can start practicing speaking to it intelligently and speaking in a language that they will understand if you talk design jargon you know for feasibility risk it probably won't be as effective and if you start talking design stuff to business stakeholders in the way you may talk to other designers that's probably not going to be effective either so you want to start practicing like speaking in a way that other people will understand and that leads me to tip number two try to figure out what types of risk is most important to what type of role so let's just take a product manager for example because they probably care about a lot of the risk that you care about however given a certain situation you know stakeholder situation or the budget or timeline certain other types of risk might be prioritized over usability now you need to be able to speak to that and also be able to speak to how your usability risk is an important factor or an overlap to everything else going on now that leads me to tip number three there's not going to be just one risk that the team cares about it's going to be a mix of risks and a different priority depending on the project it's your job to try to figure out what that is and be able to get the whole team to speak about the risk in the same way that's just i found it to be the best way to get the team aligned on the same page about what the problem is and how you're going to try to solve it and even then it's still difficult but i found that it just makes it way easier and it's pretty effective.

## 3. Мелкие UX-компромиссы копятся; покажи будущее состояние
`процесс · крафт · дизайн-система` · [07:28](https://youtu.be/B9ZdpeoPZ7A?t=448)

**Мысль:** Мелкие решения (не тот цвет, кнопка чуть в стороне) складываются в долг; в контексте долгосрочного видения их эффект виден, а решения сегодня готовят завтрашний продукт.

**Слова автора:**

> Align on a future vision and designers i think this one may sting a little bit but hear me out no one intentionally sets out to make a product of terrible ux it's it happens because there's real pressure to meet business outcomes and in those situations usability things can typically get overlooked or it's very easy to overlook them especially with the iterative nature of design and building these days it's even for designers i think it's hard to see what the big deal is of using an off-brand color or just rearranging things to squeeze stuff in or just placing a button slightly somewhere else just to get the job done but you and i know that you know those things compound over time and the effects of it the negative effects of it'll show in the medium to long term but the here and now it may not make a major difference and it may seem completely fine look so it's easier to see the impact of the smaller ux decisions if you place it in context of a more holistic and longer term vision and you can use your design skills to kind of draw that up and help people see what you're talking about because typically non-designer roles they probably have a hard time imagining what you're seeing as designers it may be easier for us and more natural to be able to piece things together and imagine what things could be but that's not true for a lot of other roles so this is where you can really be proactive and help drive alignment and strategy for the product and project that you're working on the other thing i'll mention is that then the usability risks become way more apparent when you're able to see the small little decisions and the impact of it in a future state so oftentimes do showing people what a decision may become is extremely powerful this is also going to be helpful for you as a designer because this is sort of playing chess with the future state of the product or feature that you may be working on so what i mean by this is the decision that you would make here and now just to solve the problem right now will likely be very different from a decision you would make to set yourself up to have the product become something else in the future so by doing this exercise and having that holistic vision the north star of where you're trying to go it's way easier to figure out what types of decisions you need to make sure that you have so that you set yourself up well.

**Для Напарника:** Держать north star Напарника (остров растёт, кот рядом) и сверять мелкие правки с ним, как уже делают жёсткие константы визуального языка.

## 4. Принципы важнее пикселей: держись за ядро, гибок в деталях

**Мысль:** В кросс-функциональной работе спорить о деталях бесполезно; нужно жёстко удерживать базовые концепции и ментальные модели, а цвет меню или типографику уступить (пример с закусочной и суши).

**Слова автора:**

> Way more but hear me out because i really think this one's going to be helpful so amongst designers it's really helpful to talk about pixels it's rather productive and fun to do so iconography you know typography rounded corners placement of things is someone going to be confused about it how do they feel about it will they know how to use it if i design it that sort of thing and that's all important but in a cross-functional setting it's probably not going to be effective if you harp too hard on those things it's because when it comes to actually solving a problem for you know the product the feature for the business it's going to be way broader than just the usability risk so keep the pixels in mind for sure but when you're working cross-functionally you want to be flexible with the solution anchor hard on the principles like core concepts mental models that you really cannot mess up because if you do mess up on those things it changes the whole purpose of the solution because that's going to really determine the outcome of you know whatever solution you're working on so let's use a non-digital example to illustrate what i'm talking about here so if you're creating a menu for a local diner you might think of adding classic american breakfast items and staple lunch sandwiches but you probably wouldn't consider adding in a section for sushi just because your cook used to work at a sushi restaurant or because you know that sushi probably could charge more and make more money from because it just doesn't fit with the whole idea of what a diner is no way but maybe you can agree to disagree on the color of the menu or the typography and how it folds and the material that you use because that doesn't take away from the main purpose of what you're trying to do.

**Для Напарника:** Для Напарника ядро: присутствие, тёплый тон, без стыда; всё остальное можно обсуждать.

## 5. Мысли системами: пакет решений важнее одной детали
`дизайн-система · процесс` · [11:36](https://youtu.be/B9ZdpeoPZ7A?t=696)

**Мысль:** Влияние продукта определяется совокупностью решений, а не отдельным экраном; пример IKEA: система ящиков вместо одного. Три вопроса: связанные проблемы, срок жизни фичи, повторное использование.

**Слова автора:**

> Precedence it's very natural for us to really just focus on the here and now and the problem that we're given and as makers and doers as craft people i know we want to sweat the details for every single problem that comes our way but i would argue that for an evolving digital product the impact on the product is really defined by the collection or the package of solutions that you design versus it being defined by any single solution that you've created so this is a nudge for us to maybe let go of our grip on you know perfecting every single detail for every single problem but thinking a little bigger and more systematically and thinking about for any project that we're working on to consider all the related problems and see if we could come up with a solution that addresses some or all of the overlaps let's run through an example to better illustrate this so let's take ikea for example so you could have a designer just create one single storage bin right and it probably would be a very good storage bin it would get the job done however compare that to having a whole system of storage thinking about it as a whole collection of different types of bins and then designing a bin for that system and you can see the benefits of it immediately so first for you as a designer you're creating the system so you're creating like a cohesive user experience where all these different parts can now work well together the customer is gonna benefit because they're gonna probably have something more cohesive and uniform for their home and the business will probably benefit because instead of selling one storage bin you can sell a whole system of them hope that makes sense now before we move on i know this is still probably a bit nebulous so i'll just run through a couple of questions that i ask myself as i'm trying to think this way and train myself to think this way so whenever i'm working on anything i'll ask what are the related problems and how does my design impact them or you know how long will this feature be around after i design it no what are the chances that you know i may need to actually remove it and what are the chances that another part of the product is gonna need or benefit from the same exact thing that i'm making right now.

**Для Напарника:** Новые элементы (ориентиры острова, реплики, чипы) проектировать как часть системы, а не одноразово.

## 6. Всегда показывай несколько вариантов: три, два полярных и средний
`процесс` · [13:45](https://youtu.be/B9ZdpeoPZ7A?t=825)

**Мысль:** Контролируешь только мыслительный процесс; набор вариантов с плюсами и минусами вовлекает команду. Больше трёх перегружает: два противоположных и один посередине.

**Слова автора:**

> Present more than one possibility there is no way that you're going to control other people's perception of your work or that you're going to have absolute control over the precise solution that gets implemented what you do have complete control over though is your thought process and how you articulate that to other people and you want to anchor hard into what you can control so over time i've gotten in the habit of always presenting a set of solutions then walking the teams through the pros and cons of each option as well as the essence of how each option is going to solve the particular problem and anecdotally this has just always led to better design and product outcomes although it definitely takes more work and you kind of have to let go of a lot of things that you probably hold near and dear and just trust that the team is going to make better decisions this way and it's just better for brainstorming it invites people into your design process people are more engaged it's just better for cross-functional collaboration which is really at the core of good problem solving to help you get started below are some questions that you can ask yourself as you try to come up with a set of solutions so let's start with the top one you know what if we integrated with another tool instead of like building and designing something of our own what would that look like you know what would be the easiest for us to build versus what would be the fastest thing for us to build what would be the simplest experience for the user like the absolute best and what would set us up better for a future vision of the product and typically like three options is usually good for some reason anything beyond three i notice that people you know get overwhelmed and often what you wanna do is have two like polar opposites and then one that kind of sits in between what i mean by that is like two very different options and then one that kind of cuts the difference between the two.

**Для Напарника:** Для собственных дизайн-ревью Напарника: подавать три варианта (самый простой, самый быстрый, самый перспективный).
