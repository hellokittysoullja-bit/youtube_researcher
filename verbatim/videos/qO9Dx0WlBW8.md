# Getting Started with MICROCOPY & UX WRITING

**Канал:** Amruta · 7 мин · субтитры: en (ручные) · https://youtu.be/qO9Dx0WlBW8

> «Слова автора» — дословный текст из транскрипта (убраны только um/uh и заикания; в авто-субтитрах мало знаков препинания). «Мысль» и «Для Напарника» — вывод разметчика, это не речь автора.


---

## 1. Прежде чем писать слова, пойми задачу и эмоцию человека в этот момент
`микрокопирайтинг · тексты · чат · стыд` · [02:17](https://youtu.be/qO9Dx0WlBW8?t=137)

**Мысль:** На новой фиче человек любопытен или скептичен, а при ошибке он раздражён и не хочет болтливой бодрости, ему нужна профессиональная помощь, чтобы продолжить.

**Слова автора:**

> Now when I get that message, it is very tempting to get right into the words. That is, figure out which words we should use and how to phrase them. But I realized that's not the most efficient way to write the microcopy. What I need to do is step back and look at the big picture. Look at the user experience. Think through what the user is trying to accomplish in that moment. Think about what emotions the user is feeling at that moment. For example, if you are introducing a new feature, the user is probably curious to try out the new feature. Or they are excited about it, or they skeptical about it. So I need to think through what's the user's state of mind at that point and then choose the words that match that emotion and the task they are trying to accomplish. Whereas if it's an error message, then the user is probably in a frustrated frame of mind. And they do not want a chatty, enthusiastic microcopy. What they want is professional help to solve their problem and move on with their task.

**Для Напарника:** Тон реплик кота подбирать по моменту: старт (тихо и тепло), сбой (спокойно и по делу, без шуток), финал (радость).

## 2. Canvas микротекста: кто, цель, эмоции, контекст, действие, тон, критерий успеха, варианты текста
`микрокопирайтинг · процесс · тексты` · [03:33](https://youtu.be/qO9Dx0WlBW8?t=213)

**Мысль:** Вопросы по каждому экрану (есть ли полное внимание, какое действие ждём, какой тон) снимают ментальную нагрузку, а пункт «эксперименты с текстом» заставляет писать несколько вариантов.

**Слова автора:**

> The canvas helps me think through every aspect of the microcopy for every screen. For example, who's the target user? What's the purpose of the copy? What are the user's concerns and emotions at that moment? What is the user context? Do I have their full attention or do I need to highlight something to get their attention? What is the action I expect the users to take based on the microcopy? What should be the style and tone of the words that I choose? What is the success criteria? And my favorite part of the canvas – the copy experiments. Since I was new to the UX Writing process, I used to come up with like one version of the copy, and be done with it. But having this prompt to think through the copy experiments made me want to come up with different versions of the microcopy and then choose the one that best met my requirements. So that is Step 1 of my UX Writing process – step back, fill out the canvas, and think through the user experience for each screen.

**Для Напарника:** Для каждой новой строки Напарника держать мини-карточку: момент, эмоция, действие, тон, 2–3 варианта.

## 3. Глаголы с точки зрения пользователя, у каждого слова своя работа, «кратко» значит «экономно»
`микрокопирайтинг · тексты · доступность` · [04:46](https://youtu.be/qO9Dx0WlBW8?t=286)

**Мысль:** Ошибку лучше переформулировать как понятное действие («неверный пароль»), важное в начало фразы, остальное безжалостно резать; но достаточно слов, чтобы человек понимал последствия.

**Слова автора:**

> "Pay attention to the verbs" Rephrase the action so that it is from the user's point of view and not from the system's point of view. Which means, replace the technical jargon with words that the user is familiar with. For example, consider an error message which says something like "Failure: An authentication error has occurred". This is from the system's point of view. The user doesn't know what to do about it. So to make it more clear to the user, you can rephrase the error message as "Sign-in error: You have entered an incorrect password." So in this case, the user knows what the error is, where they went wrong, and more importantly, how to correct it and move forward. It's more actionable guidance for the user. Another of my favorite points from the video is that make sure every word on the UI has a distinct job. If not, get rid of it. Keep the most important information or keywords at the beginning of the sentence, and then ruthlessly edit everything that comes after it. This is a tip that I found incredibly useful while writing concise and clear microcopy. And my favorite tip from the video is "concise" does not mean "short"; it means "economical". I think that's a common trap that UX writers fall into. When you are told to write microcopy for a UI, you want to keep it as short as possible. But that's not always effective and that's not always in the user's best interest. Instead, provide all the information that the user would need to make informed decisions and understand the consequences of the task that they perform.

**Для Напарника:** Сообщения об ошибках кота: что случилось, что делать дальше, в его голосе; не сокращать до потери смысла.
