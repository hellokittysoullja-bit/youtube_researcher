## 3. Фокус на одном: активные мгновенные апгрейды, а не пассивные
`игровой-дизайн · процесс · крафт` · [03:46](https://youtu.be/S-JsFaJhs88?t=226)

**Мысль:** Пассивные эффекты делали игру «мутной»; сосредоточились на активных эффектах с мгновенным откликом.

**Слова автора:**

> A Game About Feeding a Black Hole is fundamentally a very active play style where you're moving your cursor around, hovering over stuff and breaking things. You're getting upgrades, but all of those upgrades we ended up finding out had to be these active instant effects. So, electric shocking the screen or supernovas exploding in areas or laser beams or moons making all these things better. We tried out passive effects, but that made the game feel really muddy. So, we kind of really focused in on the one thing we're doing really well and then tried to deliver on that experience.

**Для Напарника:** Выбрать для Напарника один главный опыт и удалять улучшения, которые размывают отклик.

## 4. Качели силы: дай почувствовать мощь и снова подними сложность
`игровой-дизайн · мотивация · удержание` · [05:18](https://youtu.be/S-JsFaJhs88?t=318)

**Мысль:** Игрок переходит от «слишком слаб» к «одним ударом», затем вводят более крупные цели; такое повторное «разгон-торможение» ощущения силы держит интерес и не наскучивает.

**Слова автора:**

> Damage versus health. This is what A Game About Feeding a Black Hole essentially is. Is as you're playing it, you can go back and forth between being more powerful or less powerful than the things you're trying to break. Breaking bigger things that have more health give you more money, so you're incentivized and rewarded to go after them. But then on the tree, the fun thing you get to do is you get to have this push and pull effect where at one point you were able to one-shot everything, but then we pull up the health again, so now you can no longer one-shot them again. And then you can go back and forth, and so you get this teeter-tottering effect. It's what games like Path of Exile do and Diablo. It's what I think World of Warcraft does, especially in classic, where you at one point you start a new zone and you're too weak to be able to beat everything. You get a couple upgrades, and now you're able to go back and kill those same enemies that were just kicking your butt. So, doing this damage versus health effect, Shell Divers does this very well as well. You're you're DPSing these creatures, and you slowly get upgrades in the tree to be able to DPS them more and more and more, and eventually you're able to kind of one-shot stuff. And now you feel really powerful, but what do they do? They introduce a new zone where now you find bigger stuff. And the bigger stuff doesn't linearly scale with the amount of stuff. So, it's like it's this exponential thing, so you're not even you're encouraged to then be able to go and try to do this slower-paced gameplay where you're breaking bigger and bigger objects and then go find the upgrades. Yeah, where all of a sudden now you're one-shotting that stuff again. And then they introduce another tier where now it's even harder to break, and you have this nice push and pull effect, which gives you this longevity of keeping the player interested, keeping the player engaged so that they are not ever becoming too bored. And you're kind of playing with their perception of how powerful they are. If a player all of a sudden feels like a god mode and you just don't have any way to kind of push against that, especially if you're doing one of these kind of A Game About Feeding a Black Hole or kind of Shell Divers like, you need them to feel powerful, and then you need to pull that back down so that they can feel powerful again, and then you pull it back down. And it's just kind of revving the engine up and down on this player's power that ends up feeling pretty good.

**Для Напарника:** Для острова и Фокуса чередовать моменты лёгкой победы и чуть большего вызова (длиннее сессия), так чтобы вызов оставался посильным и без штрафов.

## 5. Чередуй волны крупных апгрейдов с медленными пассивными участками
`игровой-дизайн · награды · дофамин` · [07:46](https://youtu.be/S-JsFaJhs88?t=466)

**Мысль:** Нужны резкие скачки (новая способность, 100% крит) среди размеренного накопления, чтобы воспринимаемая сила игрока постоянно менялась.

**Слова автора:**

> Don't make it too formulaic. Have these things come and go in waves of these big crunchy upgrades that feel really powerful and good. And then have some moments during your upgrade tree where it's a little bit more kind of just like slowly chugging along with these passives. And then have more big spike moments where all of a sudden a new ability comes in or a new thing comes in or a big power increase comes in. Maybe they get 100% crit chance or something. Those moments can feel really impactful and powerful in a moment in a gameplay where you're kind of constantly playing with the player's perception of how powerful they are.

**Для Напарника:** Награды острова не равномерные: чередовать плавный рост и редкие яркие события (новая постройка, находка).

**Слова автора:**

**Для Напарника:** Не награждать за ручные повторные тапы или скорость: награда идёт за присутствие и завершённые шаги, а не за механическое повторение.

## 7. Новизна: «блестящая конфета» на 15-й минуте цепляет
`игровой-дизайн · удержание · дофамин` · [13:15](https://youtu.be/S-JsFaJhs88?t=795)

**Мысль:** Электрические астероиды появляются примерно на пятнадцатой минуте и цепляют игрока цепной молнией.

**Слова автора:**

> And my final tip is going to be novelty. With all of these games, all the way spanning from more active games like mine to more passive games like Paint's game Execute, you have to make sure you're introducing novelty. And what do I mean by novelty? I mean something that's new, something that grabs the player's attention that gives them that ooh shiny piece of candy. A really easy example is when we introduce electric asteroids in A Game About Feeding a Black Hole. For about the first 30 minutes, you're kind of just breaking stuff, getting upgrades, and feeling good about the game. it's actually at minute 15, I think, is where we introduce the electric mechanic where you start shocking stuff and you start doing this kind of chain lightning AoE stuff. That moment really hooks the player.

**Для Напарника:** Планировать для первой недели Напарника момент «ох, новое» (первый ориентир, первая находка) в ранние сессии.

## 8. Найди момент скуки через пробу и вопрос «хотел ли ты остановиться?»
`исследования · процесс · удержание` · [14:04](https://youtu.be/S-JsFaJhs88?t=844)

**Мысль:** Собственная проверка («съесть собственную еду») теряет чувствительность, поэтому нужны плейтестеры; ключевой вопрос: был ли момент, когда хотелось бросить. Это точка, куда нужна новизна.

**Слова автора:**

> And basically, what we tried to do for the rest of this tree is figure out when do we need to re-engage the player? When does it kind of start to feel like, "Okay, I've done this loop for enough." And you can do that by yourself as a player, play your own game, dog food it, eat the game. Does it taste good still? At what point does it not keep tasting good to you? And when do you need to add something new? Eventually, you'll lose your ability to tell if that if that novelty is there or not, and that's where play testers come in huge. Ideally, you can watch them in person play your game and just kind of ask them, "Hey, a really good question. Was there any point that you wanted to stop playing the game?" A lot of the times I'll be playing incremental games and play testing them, and I want to stop pretty quickly, which just means the main mechanic doesn't align with me. There's just some friction or some cumbersomeness or some weird stuff about it that I didn't like, or I'll get 15 minutes in and kind of get bored by it. And that is where, my friend, novelty comes into play.

**Для Напарника:** В интервью с пользователями Напарника спрашивать, в какой момент хотелось закрыть приложение, и добавлять новизну именно туда.

## 9. Малая новизна: шанс крита, временный бонус
`игровой-дизайн · дофамин · награды` · [15:02](https://youtu.be/S-JsFaJhs88?t=902)

**Мысль:** Новинка не обязана быть глобальной: достаточно шанса крита или плавающего усилителя, дающего ощущение суперзаряда на 10 секунд.

**Слова автора:**

> As soon as somebody starts to get bored, you got a few options. One, you can add a new mechanic or a new twist that slightly changes their game play. It doesn't have to be the zoom out and the world's a big and there's this and there's that. It can be something really minor. Like all of a sudden there's a chance to crit. Or there's this little power-up that floats around and when you break it, your DPS increases for 10 seconds and it feels like this fun supercharging moment.

**Для Напарника:** Малые сюрпризы в сессиях: редкая тёплая реплика кота или светлячок, дающие приятный всплеск.

**Слова автора:**

> And maybe if the game gets if it gets long enough, you maybe have to do some more drastic novelty or what Thorn and I call breaking of the contract with the player. We probably would have had to do something where we broke the contract with the player and maybe now introduce a mega click where they can click and hold and go and you get this crazy big clicking moment or something to break the player the contract with the player in a different way that would let us kind of do something different. I'm not saying do that for your game. I think if you get to the point where you've explored all the mechanics and you really don't you're like, "I just I don't know what else to add, Aramis." I think at that point you can call that game done. Wrap it up, package it up. If the game's an hour or 2 long, that's perfectly fine. Price it at 4 to 5 bucks, discount it at 30% when it launches. Be in the next fest, get your stuff done on the itch page so you get the validation. Make sure those first 5 minutes are nice and tight and clean.

**Для Напарника:** Не раздувать Напарника лишними режимами, когда идеи исчерпаны; лучше шлифовать первые минуты.
