## 1. Calm technology: дефицит — внимание, не устройства
`внимание · этика · уведомления` · [01:32](https://youtu.be/jXlDWeY9uYw?t=92)

**Мысль:** Через 50 млрд устройств главный дефицит века — внимание. Технология должна делать работу за человека и звать его только когда необходимо.

**Слова автора:**

> The idea behind calm technology is that we're going to have at least 50 billion devices by 2020, and that the scare resource in this century is not going to be our devices, it will be our attention. That is the thing the least amount of, and so how can devices be built in a way that allows us to go back to being more human? We talk about automation all the time, but we don't want to automate hanging out with friends and family, or watching sunset. We want to have computers do things for us and alert us to them only when necessary, not when unnecessary.

**Для Напарника:** Кот не должен тянуть внимание: подаёт голос только по событию (конец сессии, находка), а не по таймеру. Согласуется с принципом контингентности.

## 2. Чайник и Roomba: сигнал без слов
`звук · уведомления · маскот` · [02:21](https://youtu.be/jXlDWeY9uYw?t=141)

**Мысль:** Один свет или тон передаёт состояние без перевода; Roomba нравится, потому что издаёт мелодичный сигнал, а не говорит голосом «я закончил».

**Слова автора:**

> Starting with a tea kettle where you set it, you forget it, then it calls to you when it's done or when you can go pick it up. It's the idea of how much can you convey with a simple light or a simple tone. Why is the Roomba vacuum cleaner really liked by people? Well, because it doesn't say in a disembodied human voice, "I'm done!" It goes, "Da-da-da-da!" Or "Dun-dun." And you can tell what that means without having to translate that into so many different languages.

**Для Напарника:** Сигнал конца сессии — мягкий невербальный звук/мурлыканье кота вместо уведомления с текстом.

## 3. Разрешения — в момент нужды, с объяснением зачем
`доверие · онбординг · этика` · [03:31](https://youtu.be/jXlDWeY9uYw?t=211)

**Мысль:** Не просить всё сразу при открытии приложения: спрашивать доступ в точке, где он нужен, и говорить, для чего данные.

**Слова автора:**

> So, there's a number of different tricks that people can apply to their software so that you can do what you want with people's data, but the key is to slice it up into separate pieces so that it's not just giving somebody permission that's all or nothing. Often times it says, "We want to use your photos." Or, "We want access to your contacts." Right when you open an app. It's not like you have a choice or not, it's all or nothing. What can you do at the point of somebody sharing something or the point of somebody wanting to access photos within the app? That's when you ask for those permissions and that's when you tell people what those permissions will be used for, what that data gets used for.

**Для Напарника:** Разрешение на уведомления/звук/вибрацию просить после первой завершённой сессии голосом кота, а не при первом заходе.

## 4. Звук по умолчанию тихий, громкость повышает человек
`звук · доступность · крафт` · [05:35](https://youtu.be/jXlDWeY9uYw?t=335)

**Мысль:** 30–40% проблем у людей — не с самим устройством, а с тем, как звучит сигнал. В малом пространстве по умолчанию тихо, громче — по выбору.

**Слова автора:**

> I'm working on a book very quietly right now on sound design for products, just the sound design because what I noticed when writing the book on calm technology is that maybe 30% or 40% of people that I talk to had issues not necessarily with the object, but the way the alert sounded when they had the alert. In some cases, you want a really obnoxious alert, you want a kind of annoying alarm clock sound so that it jars you awake. You want a very, very loud fire alarm because you want it to wake somebody up, but if you have a washer or dryer that is in a small apartment or condo like a lot of people are living in today, you don't want that thing to be really noisy. You want to buy a product based on how little it sounds. When it's done, you're probably going to be in the same room with it, what happens if you come home and you want to run your laundry at three in the morning or your dishwasher? I've encountered ... What's happening a lot is people are moving from these larger houses in the suburbs to these smaller places, and why you have really loud sounding washers and dryers and appliances in these larger houses is because they were far away. Often, the washer and dryer was in the basement and it needed to be incredibly loud so you could hear it through the floorboards. If you're in a really small place, the best thing you can do for your customers is allow a variable sound setting or by default, have it be quite low and allow people to turn it up.

**Для Напарника:** Звук костра и сигналы Напарника по умолчанию тихие, с явной регулировкой громкости.

## 5. Вибрация или звук — зависит от контекста
`хаптика · звук · доступность` · [07:53](https://youtu.be/jXlDWeY9uYw?t=473)

**Мысль:** Личный сигнал должен быть вибрацией (человеку неловко от писка на похоронах), а сигнал для опекуна — звуком; выбор всегда контекстный.

**Слова автора:**

> There's some cases in which I had an employee with an insulin pump and the insulin pump made a beep, and he was so embarrassed by it because it would beep during films, it would beep during weddings and funerals, and it couldn't turn it off. He had no choice, and in that case, you need to be able to have a buzzer, right? You need to have a haptic vibration because let's say he's at a really loud concert and he misses the beep, then he's got an insulin issue. It's a personal alert, therefore it should be a buzz. If a caretaker is taking care of somebody then by all means, that should be an audible tone so that the caretaker can take care of the person. It's all contextual, you kind of think about where things are moving and you have to kind of future-proof your products and not make them super annoying.

**Для Напарника:** Личные сигналы Напарника — хаптика, не звук; дать переключатель звук/вибрация.
