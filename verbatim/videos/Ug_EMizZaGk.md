## 1. Золотое правило: к каждой просьбе показывай, как это улучшит жизнь
`онбординг · доверие · микрокопирайтинг` · [01:56](https://youtu.be/Ug_EMizZaGk?t=116)

**Мысль:** Просьба без объяснения вызывает сопротивление; Notion, Duolingo (email, чтобы не потерять стрик), Yelp и Depop дают понятную выгоду к каждому запросу.

**Слова автора:**

> A lot of onboarding experiences just ask you for things, your email, your location, access to your data or activity. And without the proper context, it can cause hesitation or even rejection for the user. Why do you need my location? Why do you want to track activity? And most importantly, what do I as the user get in return? If users don't know why, or better yet how, something is going to benefit them, they're probably going to drop off. Notion avoids this by following what I think of as the golden rule of user onboarding. If you're asking the user to do something, you don't just have to tell them why. You have to show them how it will improve their experience. It's not just Notion that it's getting this right. Duolingo asks for your email, but only after your first lesson, and they explain that it's to save your progress so you don't lose your streak. Yelp ask for your location, but they tell you that it's to show you nearby places and events that you'll actually care about. Depop asks you to enable notifications, but explains that it's the fastest way to know when a price drops on an item you've liked. In each case, the ask comes with a clear user-centric reason to say yes.

**Для Напарника:** Каждую просьбу (имя, уведомления, звук) формулировать голосом кота с конкретной выгодой для человека, без давления.

## 2. Кейс Triply: убрать ранний email, добавить объясняющие вопросы, отложить регистрацию
`онбординг · активация · исследования` · [04:04](https://youtu.be/Ug_EMizZaGk?t=244)

**Мысль:** Ранняя регистрация это скоростная кочка, а рекомендации казались общими; редизайн задаёт больше вопросов с пояснением, зачем они, и просит email только при сохранении, что вернуло уверенность.

**Слова автора:**

> But it's also got two major problems. First one, that email sign up right away is a speed bump. I wasn't sure why I needed to sign up or how it was going to create a better experience. Second, the recommendations felt generic. I only answered a few questions and I didn't feel like there was enough detail to really personalize them. I felt like this onboarding experience could use some improvement, but I wanted to validate it. So I ran a study on Lyssna. In this usability study, we ask participants to walk through that same onboarding and then answer a question at the end to gauge their confidence in those recommendations. The results and the feedback was pretty underwhelming. Sure, the process was quick, but most users weren't very confident in those results and the activities that they were being recommended. So let's talk about how we'd improve it. Can we make a few changes that will boost the confidence in those recommendations without making the onboarding process too long, tedious, or over complicated? Here's what I changed in my redesign version. I removed the email signup from the first step. Now the onboarding begins with Where are you going? Then I added a question to ask users how long they're staying, and more importantly, explained why it matters that we can use that to recommend activities that are a great fit for your trip. Next, it asks who you're traveling with, solo, partner, friends, family, and explains how this helps personalize suggestions even further. I also added some more detailed interest tags so we can give more tailored recommendations based on their actual preferences. That email signup we took out, it's still here, but now it appears when a user wants to add one of those activities to their agenda. Instead of just forcing them to create an account, we give them the benefit. Save the activities you love and keep your perfect trip plan all in one place. It's a clear value exchange and it makes the ask feel worth it for the user.

**Для Напарника:** Откладывать любые аккаунт-просьбы до момента, когда человеку есть что сохранять (первый заработанный кусочек острова).

## 3. Тестируй уверенность в результате, а не только скорость прохождения
`исследования · метрики · онбординг` · [06:30](https://youtu.be/Ug_EMizZaGk?t=390)

**Мысль:** Скорость и завершение не говорят, почувствовал ли человек, что его настроили на успех.

**Слова автора:**

> Explain the benefit with every ask. Go from, give us your info to, here's how this helps you. Finally test and refine. Go beyond completion rates or times to complete the task and use a usability study or any other method and Lyssna to dig a little bit deeper into the user experience. More important than how smooth it was or how long it took, were they confident in the end results, and did they feel set up for success?

**Для Напарника:** При проверке первой сессии спрашивать не «сколько заняло», а «чувствовал ли, что получится»; в тоне без оценок.
