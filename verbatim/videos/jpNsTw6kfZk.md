## 1. Стоп-сигналы: конец главы, серии, страницы; бесконечный скролл их стёр
`этика · внимание · время` · [01:28](https://youtu.be/jpNsTw6kfZk?t=88)

**Мысль:** Раньше у медиа были встроенные точки решения «хочу ещё?»; бесконечный скролл (Раскин) и автоплей убрали их, оставив решение усталому мозгу в полночь.

**Слова автора:**

> Humans rely on outside cues to know when to stop. The end of a chapter, the end of an episode, the bottom of a bowl. Psychologists call these stopping cues, natural moments where your brain gets to ask, "Do I want more?" For almost all of history, media had them built in. The evening paper ran out of pages. The TV broadcast ended for the night. Even the first websites made you click through to page two. That tiny click was a decision point. Most of the time you clicked anyway, but at least you were asked. Then, in the mid-2000s, a designer named Aza Raskin created infinite scroll. New content simply loads as you approach the bottom. It wasn't built to trap anyone. It was built to save your click. Convenient, yes, but it deleted the stopping cue. The choice to stop is never offered, so you have to invent it yourself, tired at midnight. >> Raskin has since said he regrets how his invention came to be used. Autoplay does the same for video. The next episode starts before you've decided you want it. >> Stories and short videos follow the same rule. When one ends, the next is already playing. So, there is never a clean edge to stop at. Mechanism one, remove every natural reason to stop.

**Для Напарника:** Вернуть края: у сессии есть явный конец, у дня — вечерний итог, после которого кот предлагает остановиться.

## 2. Переменный график: не награда, а неопределённость держит
`дофамин · награды · этика` · [02:44](https://youtu.be/jpNsTw6kfZk?t=164)

**Мысль:** Нерегулярное вознаграждение вызывает навязчивое поведение; feed и уведомления работают как слот-машина.

**Слова автора:**

> The psychologist Skinner found something counterintuitive about rewards. A pigeon fed every time it pecked a key, pecked steadily and quit fast when the food stopped. But, a pigeon fed unpredictably, sometimes after two pecks, sometimes after 20, pecked frantically and kept going long after the food ran out. That's a variable reward schedule, one of the most robust findings in behavioral psychology. It's also how a slot machine works. Now, look at your feed. Most posts are forgettable, but every so often something is genuinely great, and you never know which swipe it'll be. Even the gesture echoes the machine. Loren Brichter, who invented pull to refresh, has compared it to pulling a slot machine lever. Notifications work the same way. Most are nothing. A few matter. So, every buzz gets checked. Notice what's missing here. Nobody designed each post to be great. The feed only needs the occasional great one scattered at random to keep you pulling. Here's the twist. The reward itself isn't what keeps you going. It's the not knowing, and that brings us to your brain.

**Для Напарника:** Не использовать непредсказуемые уведомления; редкие находки на острове привязаны к сессиям.

## 3. Дофамин — это «хочу», а не «нравится»
`дофамин · нейро · исследования` · [03:51](https://youtu.be/jpNsTw6kfZk?t=231)

**Мысль:** Нейроны Шульца реагируют на предсказание награды; Берридж различает wanting и liking; популярное утверждение «как наркотики» выходит за пределы доказательств.

**Слова автора:**

> You've heard that scrolling gives you hits of dopamine. That's only half right. In the 1990s, the neuroscientist Wolfram Schultz recorded dopamine neurons in monkeys. They fired when a reward arrived unexpectedly. But once a light predicted the reward, they fired at the light instead. And when the expected reward didn't come, their activity dipped. So, dopamine tracks possibility. Something good might be coming. Pay attention. Researchers like Kent Berridge call this the difference between wanting and liking. Dopamine drives the wanting. The enjoyment runs largely on other systems. Now, combine this with mechanism two. An unpredictable feed keeps your prediction system switched on. Because every swipe is a small maybe. It's also why the buzz of a notification can feel more exciting than the message waiting behind it. That's the feeling from the start of this video. You can want the next clip intensely while barely enjoying the one in front of you. Lots of chasing, very little catching. One honesty check, though. The popular claim that phones flood your brain with dopamine the way drugs do goes well beyond the evidence.

**Для Напарника:** Награда в Напарнике должна давать и удовольствие от завершения (liking), а не только предвкушение; не обещать «дофаминовый взрыв» как нейронауку.

## 4. Социальные долги: стрики и прочитанные сообщения
`стрики · этика · тёмные-паттерны` · [05:53](https://youtu.be/jpNsTw6kfZk?t=353)

**Мысль:** Лайки приходят на переменном графике; стрик и непрочитанное создают долг, из-за которого открываешь приложение без причины.

**Слова автора:**

> We are social animals. For most of human history, being accepted by the group was a matter of survival. And our brain still track approval closely. Likes, comments, and views turn those old signals into live numbers. And they arrive, of course, on a variable schedule. Three reactions or 300, you check, then check again. Add the fear of missing a group chat or a story everyone else already knows, and you have a reason to open the app even when nothing is happening. Some apps add small social debts on top, a streak you don't want to break. A message marked as read that now feels like it needs an answer. And the people posting are chasing the same numbers you are. So, the post themselves are shaped to grab you in the first second.

**Для Напарника:** Нет стриков с потерей, нет «прочитано/ждёт ответа» на реплики кота.

## 5. «Захвачен», а не «болен»: привычка на автопилоте
`этика · доверие · стыд` · [07:34](https://youtu.be/jpNsTw6kfZk?t=454)

**Мысль:** Для большинства точнее слово captured: привычка, которая стоит больше, чем даёт, стоит исправления и без диагноза.

**Слова автора:**

> Clinically, social media addiction isn't an official diagnosis. The World Health Organization does recognize gaming disorder, and researchers are debating whether heavy social media use belongs in the same family. For minority of people, the harm to sleep, work, and relationships is real. Signs worth taking seriously: losing sleep most nights, falling behind at work or school, or failing to cut back even when you genuinely try. If that list sounds like you, it's worth talking to a doctor or a therapist, not just changing your settings. For most of us, a better word is captured. A habit on autopilot that costs more than it gives, worth fixing even if it isn't a disease.

**Для Напарника:** Формулировать без патологизации и стыда: «привычка», «автопилот», не «зависимость».

## 6. Воля слабее среды; заранее задать точку остановки (if-then)
`привычки · запуск-задачи · время` · [08:18](https://youtu.be/jpNsTw6kfZk?t=498)

**Мысль:** Волю используют, когда продукт сильнее всего; трение, отключение лишних пингов и заранее выбранная точка остановки работают надёжнее.

**Слова автора:**

> Willpower is your weakest tool because you use it exactly when the product is strongest. So, change the environment instead. One, add friction. Make the apps you overuse harder to reach. Small increases in effort reliably reduce automatic habits. Put the app in a folder on your last screen, or sign out. So, getting back in takes a password. Every extra tap is a stopping cue you've put back. Two, cut the random pings. Turn off every notification that isn't from a person you actually need. Keep calls and messages from real people. Everything else can wait until you choose to look. In one field experiment, people who silenced notifications for a week reported less inattention. Three, set your stopping point before you start. One episode, 10 minutes, the end of the bus ride. These simple if-then plans, called implementation intentions, reliably help people follow through.

**Для Напарника:** Перед сессией кот спрашивает «когда закончим?» и подсказывает «если таймер прозвенел, то встаю».

## 7. Потерянный час — не моральный провал; вернуть края
`стыд · этика · время` · [09:34](https://youtu.be/jpNsTw6kfZk?t=574)

**Мысль:** Машина, созданная ловить внимание, встретила мозг, созданный быть пойманным; помогает вернуть искусственные края.

**Слова автора:**

> That hour you lost last night wasn't a moral failure. It was a machine built to capture attention meeting a brain built to be captured. Once you can see the machine, it's a little easier to step out of it. Try one of those three changes this week and watch what happens to that midnight hour. You don't have to quit anything. You just have to put a few edges back where the designers took them away.

**Для Напарника:** Тон возврата после срыва: «это не провал», плюс конкретный край (таймер, итог дня).
