## 1. История Раскина: добрая цель убрать трение превратилась в бесконечную ленту
`этика · тёмные-паттерны · крафт` · [00:23](https://youtu.be/hdeMzI70NOs?t=23)

**Мысль:** Дизайнер убирал мелкое препятствие ради «непрерывности», но убрал и точку остановки; благие намерения не отменяют последствий.

**Слова автора:**

> Aza Raskin was 22 years old when he set out to fix what he saw as a clumsy part of web browsing. In 2006, while working at a small design firm called Humanized, he wrote a blog post outlining a new way to move through information. Instead of clicking a button to load the next page, the site would simply reveal more content as the user scrolled. The idea was simple, remove a tiny obstacle, make things smoother. Raskin's early prototypes quietly appeared on experimental sites, each one shaving off another moment of friction. Dig, an early social news aggregator, and even Facebook's news feed began to test the lazy load approach. By 2009, the technique was packaged up as a jQuery plugin called infinite scroll, ready for anyone to add to their site with a few lines of code. At first, the change felt like progress. No more waiting for a page to reload, no more breaking the flow to hunt for a tiny button. Raskin believed he was making the web friendlier, more human. He later described his goal as user-friendly continuity, a way to keep people in the zone, not stuck in a cycle of clicking and waiting. There was no grand plan to keep users trapped. The feature spread quickly, adopted by news sites, blogs, and social networks eager to keep visitors engaged. The technical side was straightforward. A bit of JavaScript would watch as the scroll bar neared the bottom, then quietly fetch the next batch of content in the background. The user just kept scrolling, unaware of the machinery running beneath the surface. But the consequences were bigger than anyone imagined. Years later, Raskin would look back with regret. In interviews and even court testimony, he estimated that infinite scroll now consumes 200,000 human lifetimes every single day. He described feeling responsible for a system that makes it almost impossible to stop.

## 2. Бесконечная лента: +27% просмотренного контента против пагинации
`дофамин · метрики · тёмные-паттерны` · [05:32](https://youtu.be/hdeMzI70NOs?t=332)

**Мысль:** Исследование 2011 г. показало рост просмотра на 27% при бесконечной ленте; объясняется переменным подкреплением.

**Слова автора:**

> Each swipe on an endless feed is more than a simple gesture. It taps into a reward system that behavioral psychologists have compared to a slot machine. The brain craves novelty and surprise, and infinite scroll delivers both through unpredictable bursts of content. Sometimes the next post is a meme, sometimes it is breaking news. Sometimes it is nothing at all. This uncertainty keeps the dopamine flowing, encouraging users to keep scrolling in search of the next hit. A 2011 study measured a 27% increase in the amount of content viewed when users faced a never-ending feed compared to traditional pagination. This effect is explained by what researchers call a variable ratio schedule, the same principle that keeps gamblers pulling the lever, never knowing when the next reward will come.
