## 1. Действие: проще сделать продукт, чем поднять мотивацию (Фогг в Hooked)
`активация · запуск-задачи · онбординг` · [04:22](https://youtu.be/az-VpnXi-FM?t=262)

**Мысль:** Поведение требует триггера, мотивации и способности одновременно; улучшать способность дешевле, чем мотивацию, и короткая регистрация часто эффективнее рекламы.

**Слова автора:**

> Step two, the action. You'd think a trigger would be enough. Eyal says it isn't. He leans on a model from the Stanford researcher B.J. Fogg. A person acts only when three things show up at once. A trigger, enough motivation, and the ability to actually do the thing. Without the other two, the trigger goes nowhere. If a company can only improve one of them, Eyal says, "Make the product easier to use. Raising motivation is hard and expensive. Removing steps is cheap. A shorter registration often does more than a cleverer ad.

**Для Напарника:** Каждый шаг старта в Напарнике делать минимальным (одно поле, один тап); не требовать настройки до первой пользы.

## 2. Три вида награды (племя, охота, себя) и награда должна совпадать с причиной прихода
`награды · дофамин · мотивация` · [05:08](https://youtu.be/az-VpnXi-FM?t=308)

**Мысль:** Переменные награды усиливают тягу; награды делятся на социальные, ресурсные и личного удовлетворения; деньги провалились там, где люди приходили за уважением.

**Слова автора:**

> Step three, the reward. A trigger gets you to try something. You'll only come back if product reliably delivers what it promised. That's the flaw. Eyal says the real pull comes from rewards that vary. He cites studies suggesting that wanting a reward can stir us up more than getting it. Anticipation does a lot of the work. When the reward is predictable, the excitement fades fast. When it's unpredictable, the drive gets stronger. And Eyal says that variability changes our brain chemistry in a way that keeps us chasing, which is why scrolling feed works so well. Every pull on the Twitter feed could bring news, a joke, a reply, or nothing much. You don't know which, so you pull again. Eyal sorts these rewards into three kinds: social ones like likes and friend requests. He calls them rewards of the tribe, resources like a payout from a slot machine, rewards of the hunt, and personal satisfaction like finally beating a hard level in a game, rewards of the self. The products that hook us hardest tend to mix them. The reward also has to match the reason people came. Eyal describes question and answer sites where people answered for the respect of others. Some sites tried paying them cash instead. It flopped because money was never why they'd shown up.

**Для Напарника:** Награда за сессию должна соответствовать мотиву «я сделал(а) это» (награды себя): рост острова, реплика кота, а не абстрактные очки.

## 3. Инвестиция: небольшой вклад в конце оставляет «что-то ждущее» на завтра
`удержание · привычки · активация` · [06:34](https://youtu.be/az-VpnXi-FM?t=394)

**Мысль:** Вложенные усилия повышают ценность продукта, консистентность и сдвигают вкусы; шаг «выбери тему на завтра» создаёт будущий триггер.

**Слова автора:**

> Step four, the investment. This is the step that sends people back to the start. When users put something into a product, whether that's time, effort, money, or their personal data, they're more likely to come back. Eyal gives three reasons. One, we value things more when we've put work into them. In one study he cites, people rated things they'd made with their own hands above similar things made by someone else. Spend months building up your friends list, and the network feels worth more to you. Two, we like to stay consistent. What we did before is a pretty good guide to what we'll do next. Use something often, and you'll probably keep using it. Three, our views bend to fit what we do. Think back to your first sip of beer or wine. You probably didn't like it, but everyone around you seemed to, so you kept trying, and slowly it grew on you. Eyal says products work the same way. Once people feel invested, their tastes shift until they rely on the thing. Maya adds one small step at the end of each lesson, pick tomorrow's topic.

**Для Напарника:** Zeigarnik-механика: в конце сессии предлагать записать следующий шаг (saveStepQueue), чтобы завтра было с чего начать.

## 4. Матрица манипуляции: улучшает ли это жизнь и пользуешься ли ты сам
`этика · тёмные-паттерны · доверие` · [07:42](https://youtu.be/az-VpnXi-FM?t=462)

**Мысль:** Два вопроса отделяют помощника-фасилитатора от дилера; цель — нахождение в зоне «да» на оба.

**Слова автора:**

> The word hooked makes most of us think of addiction, and Eyal takes that seriously. If a fast food chain slipped something addictive into its burgers, we'd call that wrong. But he points out that we accept some products that technically manipulate us because they make life better. Weight Watchers changed the behavior of a lot of its customers, and those customers lost weight. So, how do you tell which side you're on? Eyal gives two questions. Does the product make users' lives better, and would you use it yourself? Answer no to both, and you're likely in the wrong. His blunt example is the drug dealer. A dealer would love hooked customers. The product doesn't improve their lives, and the dealer probably doesn't touch it. In the book, these two questions form a grid called the manipulation matrix, and the dealer sits in the worst corner. The other corners vary. You might sell something you use yourself that doesn't really improve anyone's life. Eyal says that can still count as entertainment, which is a fair thing to sell. Yes to both questions puts you where he wants product makers to be. He calls that role the facilitator.

**Для Напарника:** Этический фильтр для фич Напарника: прежде чем добавить механику вовлечения, спросить, делает ли она жизнь лучше и стали бы мы пользоваться этим сами.
