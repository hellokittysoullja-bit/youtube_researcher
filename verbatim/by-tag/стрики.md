# 🎬 Dark Patterns: Are Your Games Playing You? — Chris Wilson


### Ежедневные награды за вход и эскалирующие серии: привычка «как чистка зубов» и страх потери  ·  [03:14](https://youtu.be/OCkO8mNK3Gg?t=194)
**Мысль:** Награда за вход формирует привычку без радости от игры; эскалирующие серии с обнулением эксплуатируют неприятие потерь (потеря ~ вдвое сильнее выигрыша).

> Many live service games have daily login rewards. In behavioural psychology, when an action is followed by a reward, your brain learns to repeat it. Every day that you log in and receive this reward, you get a hit of dopamine, building a habit over time. Players who are conditioned to logging in every day do it not because they’re excited to play, but because it feels like brushing their teeth: just something they have to do. Some games have login streaks. This is where the rewards escalate for each sequential day you log in. When you break the streak, your rewards reset back down to the minimum level. Now, humans have a strong sense of loss aversion, where losses feel roughly twice as bad as gains feel good, and hence players will go significantly out of their way to avoid breaking these streaks. As I mentioned before, I have a friend who was on an off-the-grid camping trip and had to walk six kilometres with his laptop and a 3G modem so that he could keep a login streak going in some random game he was playing. There are even games out there that let you pay real money to restore a broken streak.

**Для Напарника:** Не применимо: стрики со штрафом сброса противоречат тону без стыда; вместо этого накопительный остров, который не откатывается.


### Плоская ежедневная награда допустима, эскалирующая серия манипулятивна  ·  [09:59](https://youtu.be/OCkO8mNK3Gg?t=599)
**Мысль:** Если пропуск стоит лишь одного дня щедрых наград и продукт честен в остальном, механика приемлема; штраф за разрыв серии — манипуляция.

> While by definition, a dark pattern is a design that benefits the developer at the player’s expense, some of the techniques mentioned in this video aren't entirely evil, and if clearly communicated and balanced fairly, wouldn't qualify as actual dark patterns. For example, it's pretty clear that escalating login streaks are quite manipulative because players really want to avoid breaking their streak, but flat daily login rewards might be more acceptable to players because the penalty for not logging in is just missing one day of rewards. If those rewards are sufficiently generous and the game is not manipulative elsewhere, players might be happy with that system. There are certainly players who really look forward to daily bonuses or special events in their favourite games, and enjoy the structure and rewards that those bring, despite the drawbacks we've talked about.

**Для Напарника:** Ежедневный бонус (если нужен) делать плоским, без штрафа за пропуск; никаких «потерянных» серий.

### Теория перспектив: потери вдвое больнее, поэтому сгорающий счётчик давит  ·  [01:47](https://youtu.be/SqpIzOTczG0?t=107)
**Мысль:** Потери ощущаются примерно вдвое сильнее эквивалентных приобретений; стрик Snapchat превратил это в продуктовую механику со счётчиком, который может исчезнуть.

> In 1979, psychologists Daniel Kahneman and Amos Tversky published a paper called prospect theory, work that would eventually reshape entire industries far beyond academic economics. Their central finding was simple to state and strange to actually live inside. Losses feel roughly twice as painful as equivalent gains feel good. Losing $20 stings more than finding $20 ever delights you. This asymmetry now has a name inside behavioral science. Loss aversion. For decades, that finding mostly sat inside academic journals and university lecture halls, cited by economists, largely unknown outside them. Then Silicon Valley found it, and Silicon Valley does not leave good research sitting on a shelf. At Stanford, researcher B.J. Fogg spent years studying what made small daily behaviors repeat automatically, running experiments inside what became known as the persuasive technology lab. Fogg's models on behavior triggers were later studied directly by early product designers at Facebook and Instagram, some of whom sat in his classroom before ever writing a line of production code. But loss aversion, on its own, is only a finding. It needed a to become an actual product feature. It needed something a person could build, something that could visibly break, something a user could watch and worry about in real time. In 2015, Snapchat shipped a small yellow flame icon that appeared next to a friend's name. The streak. Two users sending photos back and forth every single day, a counter climbing higher with no visible ceiling. Snapchat's own public messaging around the feature emphasized connection and consistency between friends.
