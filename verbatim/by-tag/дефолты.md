# 🎬 The Laws of UX for the AI Era – 19 Psychological Design Principles — Tech Forensics


### Выбери умолчание и убери меню  ·  [03:08](https://youtu.be/3fBfJLxwSy8?t=188)
**Мысль:** Инструменты с одним умолчанием и спрятанным меню работают с законом Хика, а те, что показывают все варианты каждый раз, против него.

> Then there's Hick's law from a 1952 study by William Hick and Ray Hyman. The more choices you present, the longer it takes someone to decide. Open any modern AI coding tool's model picker. 10 options. Fast model, smart model, reasoning model, cheap model, preview model. Every one of those choices adds real decision time before you've written a single line of code. The tools that let you pick a default and disappear the menu are working with the law. The ones that surface every option every time are working against it and they don't even know it.

# 🎬 The UX Psychology Behind Apps People Can’t Stop Using — uxpeak


### Умолчания вместо пустых форм: decision fatigue  ·  [00:22](https://youtu.be/2TlIg3VokY8?t=22)
**Мысль:** Каждое пустое поле это решение; опыт с джемом: 24 вкуса дали 3% покупок, 6 вкусов 30%. 70-90% пользователей не меняют дефолты, воспринимая их как рекомендацию; задача превращается из «заполни с нуля» в «поправь».

> That sounds obvious, but almost nobody does. Stop giving users blank forms. Look at this booking screen. Five completely empty fields. Every single one is a decision the user has to make before anything happens. Psychologists call this decision fatigue. The more choices you stack on someone at once, the more likely they are to make no choice at all and just leave. A study from Columbia University found that when a grocery store displayed 24 jam flavors, only 3% of people bought one. When they showed just six, the purchase rate jumped to 30%. More choices doesn't mean better. It means harder. Now look at this version. Same screen, same fields, but everything is pre-filled with the most common choices. And the button doesn't just say search. It tells you there are already 12 results waiting. Here's why this works so well. 70 to 90% of users in most products never change the default values. That's not laziness. It's trust. When you provide a smart default, users read it as a recommendation. You're saying, "This is what most people pick." And that's incredibly persuasive. The user's job shifts from "Fill this out from scratch." to "Scan and adjust what doesn't fit." That's a fundamentally easier task. So, the principle is called smart defaults. And the rule is simple. Pre-select the most common choice for every field. Don't make users think when you already know the answer.

**Для Напарника:** Подтверждает Default Effect в Фокусе: 25 минут предвыбрано как рекомендуемое; предзаполнять шаг/длительность по прошлой сессии.



# 🎬 📊 10 Usability Heuristics Explained with Real UX Examples (Nielsen Norman Group) — AI Design Professor | Nehmat Gereige


### Предотвращение ошибок: сначала дорогие, slips против mistakes  ·  [06:00](https://youtu.be/6_e1AiOZHC0?t=360)
**Мысль:** Слипы (невнимательность) гасятся ограничениями и хорошими дефолтами, ошибки-mistakes (неверная модель) устранением нагрузки на память, undo и предупреждениями; приоритет дорогим ошибкам.

> Heuristic number five is error prevention. Create good error messages. They are very important. But the best designs will carefully prevent a problem from even happening. So as much as possible, you want to eliminate the possibility of your user getting into an error state. But that's sometimes unavoidable. So when there's an error state, plan for it and create it properly. Guardrails are something that will help your users not get into errors. So on a curvy mountain road, it prevents drivers from falling off the cliff. It's a prevention. How do we create this prevention? Prioritize your effort. Prevent high-cost errors first, then little frustrations. So first start with the critical things, then the smaller frustrations. You know there are slips and mistakes. Slips are unconscious errors caused by inattention. Mistakes are more conscious errors, but they're based on a mismatch between the user's mental model and the actual design. So avoid slips by providing helpful constraints and good defaults. But prevent mistakes by removing memory burdens, supporting undo, and warning your users when there's something to avoid.

**Для Напарника:** Для людей с СДВГ слипов больше: ограничители, умолчания и undo вместо текста ошибок.
